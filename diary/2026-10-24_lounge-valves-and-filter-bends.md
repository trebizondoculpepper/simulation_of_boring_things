# Lounge filter valves and pipe bends, 2026-10-24

The owner asked for every valve in Plant Room 1 to become a solenoid valve controlled from its local mimic, then showed a filter whose front observation area was obscured and whose outlet pipe bend approached at the wrong height. The room has eleven installed valves: four per filter, the balance tank drain, and two pump discharge valves.

I gave the lounge filters radial nozzles and routed a short diagonal nipple along each shell normal before the pipe turns onto the room's straight lines. I moved the outlet's lateral bend down after its nozzle stub. The pipe crosses the filter front below its glass and hatch, then rises outside the filter footprint. The small filters' sight glasses moved to the clear side of the shell; their nameplates and badges were repositioned to avoid overlap. The port, machine-clearance and pipe-clash tests pass, and a regression assertion checks the low bend.

The valves use the existing Grundflow solenoid butterfly component. Their commands retain permanent installed-valve asset IDs. The world now saves actual valve positions separately from commands, advances their travel over four seconds, and springs them shut when Plant Room 1 loses electrical power or compressed air. The local Penfold mimic has OPEN and SHUT controls and limit lamps for all eleven, while keeping the filter pump controls. The lounge circulation derives its return flow from the pump discharge and two parallel filter paths; a deliberate waste opening sends water to waste. The existing unattended daily backwash remains when no waste valve is commanded.
