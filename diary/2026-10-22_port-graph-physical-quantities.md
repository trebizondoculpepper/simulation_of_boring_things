# Development Diary Entry - 2026-10-22

## Context

The owner asked whether every visible machine and valve could be represented as a graph node whose typed ports must be connected, then extended the question to room and brand metadata, outside supplies and sinks, and physical quantities such as voltage, current, fluid pressure, flow, and temperature. They requested a small test of a TypeScript port-graph package and a prompt for a simplest-possible coupled plant model that tracks electrical energy.

## Work

Installed `@statelyai/graph` and added an isolated test that projects the existing plant network into it, derives ports from example equipment kinds, validates incompatible media and direction, and models external gas, fresh-water, flue, and sewer boundaries. The trial leaves the production hydraulic network and world state untouched. The graph library supplies structural checks and indexed port queries; the project's physical and completeness rules need their own validator. Editing an edge through the library API keeps its adjacency index current.

Researched Modelica's connector convention: voltage, pressure, and temperature are potential or effort values; current, mass flow, and heat rate are through values; specific enthalpy and composition are carried by moving fluid. Wrote `doc/research/port-graph-physical-quantities.md` to separate installed ratings from live simulation values and show where the latter should be addressed. Wrote `doc/prompts/013-minimal-multidomain-plant.md` for a finite gas store, boiler, circulating water tank, electrical supply and energy meter, heat loss to outside air, and explicit boundaries. Wrote `doc/prompts/done/014-port-graph-architecture-review.md` for an independent review of identity, visuals, catalogue, topology, simulation, and migration.

## Outcome

The port-graph structure is feasible for the installed equipment catalogue and connection contract. It is not itself a fluid, electrical, or thermal solver. The next implementation can start with the isolated model in prompt 013, then compare its behavior with the running world before any migration.

The independent review recommended adapting the prototype around a project-owned equipment-kind and port contract. It found conflicting IDs between the current visual inventory and plant network, and visible controls whose saved positions do not yet affect network flow. The owner chose explicit game abstractions: one electrical supply line, a downward drain to an unseen sewer, and an upward flue to an unseen exhaust sink. Pool-bound pipes should exit north and main-plant-bound pipes south, with physical routes clearing gantries, doors, and staircases.

Built dev19 as a walkable demonstration of five visible machines and their graph ports, with virtual outside-air and flue endpoints. Its finite gas store, boiler, tank, pump, and electrical supply advance a deterministic model; visual controls issue commands by installed ID, while readings follow simulated state. The demo tests one-to-one visual bindings and port coverage without changing the production network or saves.

Extended the trial with the existing boiler's actual membrane-key, rocker, knob, display and lamp panel. Its discrete control model owns power, setpoint, display selection and manual firing mode; the port-graph model alone calculates fuel, heat, flow and electrical energy, then projects readings onto the panel. Optional typed control ports belong to the boiler graph node: `temperature` is readable and `setpoint` writable in °C. The local panel works with those ports unwired. A tiny quantity type carries the unit in TypeScript and at runtime: a mass-flow message cannot set the thermostat, and the write-only port cannot be read. The setpoint changes the burner demand; a satisfied thermostat puts it in standby. The next production design should make the node-kind contract and these bindings generic rather than hard-coded to the demonstration boiler.

Added a mimic node to the same graph, with seven typed incoming signals and two boolean outgoing commands. A Penfold Controls board in dev19 reads gas store level, gas flow, boiler status and flow temperature, tank temperature, pump status and accumulated electricity; its buttons command the boiler and pump through graph edges. The view draws one shared trunked ceiling bus with drops to connected devices while the individual signal routes remain abstract. The board stands near the owner's x=3.75 m, z=2.01 m viewpoint, ahead of the equipment row.

Wrote the component-triplet migration contract in `doc/research/component-triplet.md`. It defines visual, simulation and control state ownership, stable installed identity, composite child parts, physical and optional control ports, and staged migration checks. It distinguishes what dev19 currently proves from the work needed to reconcile the production catalogue, network, views and saved world.

Replaced dev19's manual gas and water valves and power isolators with the three motorized examples. The mimic now reads their actual states and sends commands over typed control edges. A lost electrical supply stops the pump, fails the gas and water valves closed, and leaves the motorized breaker at its last position. Reused the existing synthesized pump sound, following the simulated running state, and added ignition, firing, shutdown and actuator cues tied to actual state transitions. The panel was inspected at the requested dev19 viewpoint.

Replaced dev19's unlimited electrical source with a 0.04 kWh battery. The mimic shows remaining charge and cumulative electricity used. Simulation steps are bounded to one second so a large step still reaches depletion and ends with the pump stopped, boiler unpowered and spring-return valves closed. The battery lasts roughly forty seconds at the accelerated room clock under the initial load.

Retuned the machine audio after the owner's feedback on its first in-game listen. The pump's prominent pure tones were reduced and joined by independent bearing, fan and water noise; all layers now start quietly. The boiler now starts its blower before ignition, then adds low flame and flue noise with subtle modulation. Closing the gas valve cuts the flame quickly while the blower winds down. Typecheck, lint, tests, build and browser rendering passed; the project formatter was used for the new files.
