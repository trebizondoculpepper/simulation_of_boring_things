# Changelog

All notable changes to Pool Plant are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the game uses [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html): see "Versions and releases" in the README for what each kind of change means here. Add each change under Unreleased as it is made; a release moves those entries under its own version and date.

### Fixed

- Dev26's flowmeters showed spurious flows while its valves changed over with the supply off: with no head across the network, the solver's unconverged flows leaked through. With the supply off there is now no flow at all.
- A sand filter's sight glass is now a reusable component (`plant/logic/sight-glass.ts`, `plant/views/sight-glass.ts`): a two-dimensional view through the round window at the top of the bed, for filters of any size. It is modelled on what operators look for:
  - in service, clear water over a flat bed, its top crust darkening and thickening with the dirt it holds;
  - left too long, the fouled surface channels into valleys with a ridge at the wall;
  - in a backwash, the bed lifts and churns, air bubbles at first, and the water clouds brown and clears as the dirt washes out;
  - in a rinse, the bed settles back.

  Each quantity eases toward its target, so one state blends into the next. The large sand filter's window shows it live (`showGlass`), driven in Dev26 by the rig's model, and a board in Dev26 shows the five states side by side.

- Dev26 (`?room=dev26`), backwashing a sand filter (scenario 3, prompt 026): one of the Olympic pool's 3 m filters on its five-valve battery (V1 inlet, V2 outlet, V3 backwash in, V4 backwash out, V5 rinse). The valves are actuated, and a Penfold panel switches them together by its MODE selector (OFF, SERVICE, BACKWASH, RINSE), holding the supply off while they travel. The water side is solved as a network with the simulation's solver (`game/plant/five-valve-filter.ts`), so the flow through the bed goes down in service and rinse and up in a backwash. Each valve's branch has a new electromagnetic flowmeter (Kessler-Moody, its LCD showing the flow and its direction). The panel has a mimic of the five valves, the five flows, the bed's top and bottom pressures and their difference, and SUPPLY ON, VALVES MOVING and BACKWASH DUE lamps. The dirty bed backwashes clean, its waste running brown to the gully. A small filter with a top-mount multiport valve (`multiport-filter.ts`) stays as a component; the floor gully's stream can be tinted by how dirty it runs.
- Dev25 (`?room=dev25`), a tank filled and drained through one port (scenario 2, prompt 025): an IBC's single bottom port goes to a tee, with a fill leg from 3 bar mains through a manual valve and a drain leg through another to a floor gully. Which way the water goes through the port follows the valves and pressures. A small network solve at the tee (`game/plant/tank-tee.ts`) balances the mains' inflow against the flows into or out of the tank and down the drain. With the fill open it fills; with the drain open it empties by gravity; with both open most of the mains runs to the drain. Overfilled, it spills from its top and the display says so. Nothing is created or lost.
- Dev24 (`?room=dev24`), duty and standby pumps (scenario 1, prompt 024): two end-suction pump sets on a shared suction header and a common discharge header. Headers are a new kind of component, a manifold with a main end and branch stubs. A Penfold duty/standby panel runs them (`game/plant/duty-standby.ts`). The duty pump runs; if it has not started within 3 s, because its local isolator is off, say, the panel latches its fault and DUTY FAIL and starts the standby; RESET clears the fault once the pump is available. The plumber gives each pump its suction valve, its discharge non-return and isolating valves, and a local isolator. Click a non-return valve to jam it open, and water comes back through the idle pump: its local flow readout goes negative and the delivery falls. The panel shows the run and fault lamps, flow and discharge pressure, and the pumps' gauges read live. The experiment rigs share their fitting placement (`rig-fittings.ts`).
- Eight test scenarios, written as prompts 023 to 031 with their status (`doc/prompts/scenarios.md`).
- Dev23 (`?room=dev23`), draining a jacuzzi: a 1000 L IBC of water on a concrete plinth, its bottom outlet valve piped in DN50 waste to a stainless floor gully. Click the valve's lever and the tank empties by gravity (`game/plant/gravity-drain.ts`). The flow is set by the head above the pipe's open end and the run's losses, so it is fast at first and slows as the water line falls through the bottle's wall. It empties completely, in about five minutes. The drain run falls all the way at 1 in 40 and discharges over the grating with an air gap, where the water can be seen running into the gully. An LCD on the plinth shows the contents and the flow. The IBC is now a shared component (`createIbc`), which the acid tank uses too.
- Dev22 (`?room=dev22`), a battery-backed pump: an endless 450 V experimental supply, through an isolator, to a rectifier/charger, a battery bank and an inverter. These are three new components, the parts of a 40 kVA-class online UPS (`game/plant/power-conversion.ts`). From the inverter, through a second isolator, the supply goes to the end-suction pump set, which pumps water from an endless source to an endless sink. The plumber adds the isolators, the pump's suction valve and its discharge non-return and isolating valves, and routes the conduit and DN150 pipe. Isolate the supply and the battery (4.32 kWh) carries the 32 kW pump for about 7.7 minutes; then the inverter cuts out and the pump stops, restarting when the supply returns. Each cabinet's panel shows volts, amps, kilowatts and status lamps, and the battery's also shows energy (kWh), charge, voltage, current and runtime. The pump's suction and discharge gauges read live, and its local panel shows RUNNING and the flow.
- Experimental sources and sinks show their value and unit on a backlit dot-matrix display (a 16 × 2 character LCD), front and back.
- Dev21 (`?room=dev21`), source to sink: an experimental source of every substance along one wall and its sink facing it across a two-storey room. The plumber joins each source to its sink, sets a valve into each end of every line (a power isolator on power), and leaves them all closed. Open both of a line's valves and its substance flows, at the potential difference over the two resistances, capped by either's maximum rate, until the source is empty or the sink full. The lines rise straight from the studs and cross overhead, clear of the walkway. The plumber has two new options for this: `isolateMedia` (which media's lines are isolated, at both ends) and `normally` (the state its fittings are left in, open by default).
- Experimental sources (`game/plant/experimental-source.ts`): placeholders for a supply of any substance before the real plant behind it is modelled. Always drawn the same way, as a cylinder in the substance's colour banded EXPERIMENTAL, each has typed outputs (as many as asked, each carrying its substance in that substance's medium). Its one control port, `remaining`, only reports the quantity left, a number that may be infinite. It has no switch, since starting and stopping a supply is a valve's job. Every port has a precise place and outward direction in the source's own frame (`SOURCE_SHAPE`): the outputs are studs round its back, and the control port is a cable gland on top. What is drawn downstream runs it down. There is a source for every commodity: pool, mains, dirty, heating, domestic hot and chilled water; natural gas, LPG, chlorine, ozone, carbon dioxide and compressed air; hypochlorite, acid, sodium bicarbonate and flocculant; heating oil and diesel (a new `oil` medium); and 400 V three-phase, 230 V single-phase, 110 V CTE and 24 V DC power. Every source has a matching sink, with typed inputs, a capacity (which may be unlimited) and a read-only `contents` control port reporting what it holds, starting at nothing. Both have a potential (pressure, or voltage), an internal resistance and a maximum flow rate, so nothing flows at infinite speed: flow is the potential difference over the resistance, capped at the rate, and every draw or delivery over a time step is capped too. Each cylinder takes its BS 1710 identification colour and code bands (power is orange, banded in its IEC 60309 voltage colour) and has lettering painted straight onto its curved front and back (its outputs or inputs are upright studs across the top centre, its control port a gland on the back): a source or sink symbol, its name, and its rating (substance, capacity or UNLIMITED, maximum flow, resistance in ohms for power, and pressure or voltage). Dev20 (`?room=dev20`) shows a source and a sink of each.
- Components can come in variants (`DemoModel.variants`). Dev19's pool heat exchanger can be the shell-and-tube exchanger on its plinth, or a plate exchanger on the floor or on a 0.3 m plinth. The plate exchanger has all four ports at its fixed frame's corners, so its movable end opens without breaking a pipe. A new placement-scorer measure, port heights, prefers ports at a working height and marks down any below 0.3 m, which limit routing. Scored end to end, the shell-and-tube exchanger on its plinth wins: it is the only variant that passes every gate (85), and the most compact.
- A clash-free router (`voxel-router.ts`, by Codex from prompt 021) replaces the old overhead lanes. It searches a grid round the reserved regions and the lines already routed, and proves every route with the clash checker. Lines leaving or reaching the same port may share their way out, as a bus (power feeds from the mains) or a header (a pump feeding a battery of filters). The scorer counts overlaps within such a group as shared, not as clashes. The dearest pipes route first, and the flue before them all. Dev19c is the first Dev19 to pass every gate: every line routed, no clash, every fitting within reach, scoring 84. Four routing approaches were tried side by side (`router-trials.ts`: rack levels, voxel search, voxel with direction levels, rip-up and reroute) before the merge.
- Dev19's heat exchanger is now a shell-and-tube pool heat exchanger after Bowman's FG100 (`shell-tube-exchanger.ts`), in place of the plate exchanger. Pool water goes straight through its end covers, and the boiler water enters and leaves by two branches on top. It stands on a concrete plinth, so the pool line passes through it at 0.9 m and the heating pipes drop onto it from above, with nothing over it. It takes 0.6 × 0.32 m of floor against the plate exchanger's 1.3 × 0.8 m.
- Dev19d (`?room=dev19d`): Dev19's plant with three sand filters in parallel (`demo-variants.ts`), made by every stage.
  - **Blocks:** the filters are pre-assembled into candidate blocks, and a row of three in the island wins. The sizer now leaves room for the longest freestanding block to stand in the island.
  - **Headers:** where several lines share a port, such as the pool pump's discharge feeding all three filters, the plumber installs its fittings once on the common pipe, with a header node after them that the lines branch from. Flow passes through headers.
  - **Graph:** each fitting keeps one identity, and the plant runs on the plumbed graph. Shutting the pump's one discharge isolator stops all three filters.
- Placement follows three rules of process plant layout, from N. S. Nandagopal's _Practical Process Plant Layout and Piping Design_ (IDC Technologies, 2009):
  - **Process order:** equipment follows the process flow diagram, the process going one way round the room.
  - **Short pump suctions:** a suction counts double, so each pump stands by the tank it draws from.
  - **Withdrawal space:** room to draw off a pump's motor, open a plate exchanger's pack, and lift a filter's internals out.

  The placement scorer measures the first two. Dev19c is now the best of sixteen placements with these rules (seed 9). It scores 74.1 against 72.2 for the best before them, and its process order rises from 51 % to 84 %.

- A pre-assembled block of parallel units now carries its own pipework and valves. Its headers and branches are made in the shop (estimated at 60 % of the site rate), and it meets the main at a single field joint, where loose units need one each (estimated at £150). Every make-up is priced the same way: a main at the header's size to the group's middle, then a branch to each unit, every length the sum of its x, y and z runs, never a diagonal. For four filters the 2 × 2 block's own pipework costs £1,333, against £2,143 for four loose units.
- Identical units working in parallel are pre-assembled into candidate blocks before placement (`unit-blocks.ts`): for four filters, a row of 4, a 2 × 2 square (two rows back to back, standing across the island's two faces), two pairs, and so on down to singles. Each block is placed as one item and expands back into its units, side by side and aligned by construction. A block is piped to what it serves as one, through its header. The placement scorer picks the make-up, rewarding fewer placements and groups standing near what they serve. Four filters come out as a 2 × 2 square in the island, in a 13 m room against 14.5 m for two pairs.
- Pipework is priced (`pipe-costs.ts`), from a table of approximate installed costs per metre for each kind and size of pipe, conduit and flue: steel above plastic, lagged above bare, bigger above smaller. The scorer now judges pipework by what it costs, not by its length. Placement pulls machines together by what their pipe costs. A placement scorer runs straight after placement, before any plumbing, scoring access, sites, batteries, estimated pipework cost and room area. Dev19c's search places sixteen candidates, ranks them by placement score, and plumbs and fully scores only the best four. Machines of the same kind now stand together as a battery: three identical sand filters cluster in the island, where without the rule they scattered. At these estimated rates, Dev19 this morning had about £49,600 of pipework; Dev19c has about £23,900.
- A scorer, the last stage of making a plant room (`room-scorer.ts`), shaped by Codex's advice. It checks gates first: every machine reachable on foot, every fitting the rules want installed within reach, no run cutting into another, and none through a reserved region. It then scores the room from 0 to 100 on access, fittings, clashes, route economy and room area, measured against baselines the room cannot choose, saying why for each. Dev19 this morning scores 39.5; Dev19 now 66.2. Dev19c (`?room=dev19c`) is the best of twelve placements by the scorer, at 67.9. All of them still fail on pipe clashes from the old overhead lanes. The scorer also caught the boiler's power drop running down through the ring walkway, now fixed.
- The plumber now fits Dev19's valves and isolators itself (`doc/prompts/018-plumber-valves-and-isolators.md`). It adds isolating valves at pumps, filters and the heat exchanger, and a power isolator at every powered device, the boiler beyond its breaker included. It sets them and the remote valves into their lines, never on the floor, and only where someone standing on clear floor can reach them, reporting any it cannot. Each line leaves its machine first along a straight at a reachable height, long enough for its fittings. Hand valves have levers coloured for what they carry. The flue now ends at a magic ceiling terminal straight above the boiler. Dev19b (`?room=dev19b`) is the same room with the rules Codex proposed on the old overhead lanes, kept for comparison. The simulation does not yet read the fittings.
- A plant room can now be laid out by layers (`doc/prompts/017-room-sizer-gantries-placement.md`). A room sizer sets its width, length and height from what it holds; a regions helper reserves its door approach and, from two storeys up, an observation gantry and a stair against one wall, where no pipe may run; and a placement helper stands the machines in bands, backs to the walls or back to back in an island, all facing a ring walkway. The big tanks go in corners, panels and signs on walls, and pumps and vessels freestanding.
- Dev19 is the first room laid out this way: 13 × 13 m and 8 m tall instead of 20 × 20 × 4 m, with a walkable stair and gantry, yellow walkway lines and the pipe-free space shown faintly. Its piped machines stand much closer together. Its pipes are still routed by the old lanes, pending the new plumber.

- Dev19's small pool now has a post-mounted water quality sign with live graph-bound temperature, pH and free chlorine readings. Each motorized shutoff and breaker also has a local override button.
