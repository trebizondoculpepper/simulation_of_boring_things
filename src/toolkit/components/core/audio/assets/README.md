`trolley-squeak.wav` is an 8-second loop from 3.65–11.85 s of the credited shopping-cart HQ MP3 preview. Deferred loading is cancelled if movement ends before the file is ready.

Dropped-board sounds are procedural (`src/toolkit/components/core/audio/board-impact.ts`). The first collision starts one sequence of 2–5 contacts, scheduled on the Web Audio clock; subsequent physics bounces do not restart it. Approach speed controls initial loudness and biases the count: higher drops usually produce more contacts, using the actual landing speed so raised surfaces work too. Each secondary contact retains 55–67% of the previous amplitude, with a varied filter and decay. Intervals begin at 90–145 ms and shrink by a random factor of 0.65–0.8; the whole clatter lasts under half a second. A quiet 175 ms friction envelope accompanies the first landing when sliding speed exceeds 0.05 m/s. This approximates the board's edge/connector chatter beyond the coarse vertical bounce simulation.

Board clatter pitch also follows the longest physical board edge, captured before room transforms and child-card attachment. Relative to a 180 mm card, the frequency multiplier is `(0.18 / span)^0.85`, bounded to 0.4–1.6. It scales the impact band, low thump and scrape; larger boards also have slightly longer decays. This is an artistic size cue rather than a measured structural-acoustics model; size does not alter the drop-energy-dependent count or attenuation.

All pedestal devices now carry precomputed `DropProfile` settings from `src/toolkit/components/industrial-control/logic/machine-drop-profiles.ts`, including shape, span, effective radius, restitution, friction, rolling resistance, skitter and impact sound material/band/thump/decay/gain. The rendering part reads the component's profile rather than inferring sound from its current pose. Flat objects use the bounded clatter sequence; solid housings sound each actual bounce or corner contact; the cylindrical fuse cartridge has one landing sound followed by a quiet positional rolling-noise loop whose gain follows ground speed. Larger profiles lower the impact bands and thumps. These are artistic parameters, not measured material properties. The model validates every pickable object's profile; the shared handler validates them on scene setup and pickup too.

The dev6 machine sounds are the synthesised machine tones of `src/toolkit/components/sound/audio/machine-tones.ts`, with no recording assets: seamless four-second loops of hum and cooling noise, rendered once from a seed, each with its own run-up and run-down. The drive train holds the motor hum, and the buckets that would have cooling or something turning hold theirs (the control CRT and the PLC chassis fans, the drive's heatsink fan, the hydrocoptic pump and the panendermic blower), each from inside its bucket, quieter than in the dev12 listening room, while the machine has power (the incoming isolator and the main disconnect both on).

## BBC diagnostic read and Neumann Link

No new binary audio assets. `industrial-control/parts/bbc-workstation.ts` synthesises a bounded 1.3-second seek sequence inside the 1.5-second diagnostic read, positioned at the floppy drive. Paired low-level noise/body impulses suggest a 5¼-inch stepper without a continuous harsh buzz. `industrial-control/parts/neumann-diagnostics.ts` synthesises one 880 Hz, 90 ms connection beep at the internal Link Controller LED. Reads and beeps have cancellation handles; power/link loss, room suspension and disposal stop them. Resuming does not replay old activity. Ordinary telemetry changes do not trigger a read or beep. Original project synthesis, credited in `CREDITS.md`.

## BBC Micro selected melodies

The selected four resynthesized cues are in `resources/sound/effects/bbc-micro-melodies/`; obsolete cleaned/dry variants and the comparison reel are removed. Each selected cue has a light mono case response, using 80% of the earlier wet level, a 160 ms enclosure response and no stereo widening. The fanfare starts at source 3.80 s and excludes the noisy lead-in. `prepare.py`, `scores.json`, `manifest.json` and source credits document the approximation and reproducible processing.

`components/sound/audio/assets/bbc-fanfare.mp3` is the prepared fanfare, 48 kHz mono / 160 kb/s, imported by the BBC workstation part. Level0 passes the machine’s exact green-tier signal. It plays once on green if BBC power is on; a full second without green re-arms it. Power-off, green loss, suspension and disposal cancel sound. No retrospective cue on late BBC power-on, model swap or room resume.

`components/sound/audio/assets/bbc-beware.mp3` is the selected BEWARE cue (`beware-resynth.mp3`, unchanged, 48 kHz mono / 160 kb/s), played from the same speaker. Level0 passes each unit's true annunciator state (green, failed, or other) and the machine's mains. Once the machine has reached its steady state (remembered until the mains goes off, since the first failure ends it), a unit going from green to failed plays BEWARE if the BBC is on: the change, not a standing failure, and at most once every 30 s, so a cascade of failures plays it once. Nothing is queued while the BBC is off. Power-off, suspension and disposal cancel it. The two other selected cues remain in the working library until they have a game use. No original recording sample is in the synthesized assets; source melody provenance is retained in CREDITS.md.
