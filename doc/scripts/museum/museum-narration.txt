HALL 1: WARM WATER

4a. A swimming pool a huge volumen of water that is always going cold. Every instant it loses heat to the air, to the ground and to the swimmers themselves. Keeping it warm is a chain: gas burns in a boiler, hot water carries the heat to the rest of the system, and an exchanger hands it to the pool without the two water circuits ever mixing. Everything in this hall works. Turn the heating on, and watch how slowly a whole pool warms.

HALL 2: CLEAN WATER

5a. Every swimmer brings something with them: dirt, sweat, flaky skin, sun cream, and worse. The plant takes it all out again, every moment of the day. It strains the water through a bed of sand, and when the sand is full it washes the sand it out by running the water backwards. Then ozone and ultraviolet light finish the job.

5b. Watch the gauges, and you can see the filter filling up. Try running a backwash yourself - the pool technician has to do that once per day!

HALL 3: SAFE WATER

6a. Clear water is not the same as safe water. Germs are too small to see, so the plant keeps a little chlorine in the pool all the time. Too little, and the water is unsafe. Too much, and it stings. Send in a crowd of swimmers, and watch the controller keep up. Chlorination is a balancing act - the more people use the pool, the more you need to add.

HALL 5: A DAY IN THE LIFE

HALL 8: NEVER AGAIN, PUTTING SAFETY FIRST

22. Two of the chemicals that keep a pool safe must never meet. Mixed, they make chlorine gas. This is the story of a morning when they did.

23. Pellingford Baths, a spring morning in 1997. At seventeen minutes to eight a chemical tanker pulls into the loading bay. It is early. The duty technician has not yet started his shift, and the driver has never been here before.

24. Two fill points stand side by side in an open recess: one for acid, one other for sodium hypochlorite. They have the same coupling, and their labels faded long ago. The driver connects his acid hose to the hypochlorite fill. Four hundred litres of acid go into the bulk hypochlorite tank.

25. Mixed, acid and hypochlorite give off chlorine: a choking, yellow-green gas, heavier than air. It rises from the tank's vent into the plant room, and then the air handling draws it towards the pool hall. The plant room's gas detector should have raised the alarm. But, it had been disconnected, weeks before, after a series of false alarms.

26. The pool was cleared - Swimmers and staff stand in the car park in foil blankets while fire crews in gas-tight suits go in. A handful were treated for breathing difficulties.

27. Weeks later, the inspectors report. There was no risk assessment for deliveries. The staff had not been trained to handle the chemicals. Nobody had checked the contractor. The council was fined forty thousand pounds, and Pellingdford Baths were shut for six weeks.

28. Pellingford Baths reopened with its fill points rebuilt: a different coupling for each chemical, each in its own locked cabinet, clearly labelled. The gas detector works, and the stores are apart. The fix is to make the mistake impossible.
