## Lectern 1: The swimmers

Headline: The swimmers

Title: Swimmers · M3-BA1

Body: Every swimmer brings sweat, skin and worse: about 3.7 g of chlorine demand each. Chlorine destroys it, and is used up doing so. NORMAL is a public day of 600 swimmers, busiest in the evening; CROWD sends 100 in for the next hour.

Did you know?: The pool smell is not chlorine: it is chloramines, and it means the water has more dirt than its chlorine can deal with.

Try it: Send in a crowd and watch the chlorine dip and the controller answer.

## Lectern 2: The sample

Headline: The sample

Title: Sample cell · M3-SC1

Body: A trickle of the pool, about 40 litres an hour, runs past two probes all day: free chlorine AE1 and pH AE2. The float FS1 shows it is flowing; without flow the controller stops dosing after 120 seconds, because it can no longer trust what it reads.

Did you know?: Standing still, the probe uses up the chlorine in its own little cell in a few minutes: it reads low while the pool is fine.

Try it: Shut the sample valve SC1-V1: watch the lamp go out, the reading fall, and the controller stop. Open it again.

## Lectern 3: The controller

Headline: The controller

Title: Dosing controller · M3-DC1

Body: It compares what it measures with what it is set to, and doses in proportion to the difference: set to 1.3 mg/l, full output 0.5 below it, and a little more the longer it stays low (integral 6 hours). pH is set to 7.3. Two hours at full output and it stops itself (E dt): something must be wrong.

Did you know?: pH decides how strong chlorine is: at pH 7.3 about 59 % of it is the strong kind, at 7.6 only 42 %. A dose takes over an hour to reach the far side of the pool, so the controller must not panic.

Try it: Press ◄► to SEt, XP or tn, ↵ to change, ▲ ▼, ↵ to keep. ↵ on the reading acknowledges an alarm.

## Lectern 4: The pumps

Headline: The pumps

Title: Hypochlorite dosing pump · M3-DP1; Acid dosing pump · M3-DP2

Body: Each stroke pushes out a fraction of a millilitre. The hypochlorite pump gives up to 7.5 litres an hour, the acid pump 2.1. Hypochlorite pushes the pH up, so acid is dosed too, at its own injection point.

Did you know?: A bubble in the pump’s head and it strokes away delivering nothing: the plant keeps running and the water slowly gets worse.

Try it: Watch the stroke lamp speed up as the chlorine falls. A pump showing its fault: open its bleed valve DP1-V1 for 5 seconds, shut it, and press START on the pump.

## Lectern 5: The tanks

Headline: The tanks

Title: Hypochlorite day tank · M3-DT1; Acid day tank · M3-DT2

Body: Each day tank holds 60 litres, a few days’ chemical, read on its sight gauge (LI1, LI2). Refill it from its store by holding its fill valve open.

Did you know?: Never let acid and hypochlorite meet: they make chlorine gas. That is why they have separate tanks, pumps, colours and injection points.

Try it: Hold DT1-V1 open to fill the hypochlorite tank.

## Lectern 6: The test kit

Headline: The test kit

Title: DPD test kit · M3-TK1

Body: Never trust a probe you have not checked. DPD 1 turns pink with free chlorine; DPD 3 shows the total, and the difference is the combined chlorine; phenol red gives the pH.

Did you know?: Pools test by hand at least three times a day, and check the controller against the test every day.

Try it: Press TEST and compare with the controller.

## Lectern 7: When it goes wrong

Headline: When it goes wrong

Title: Fault pedestal · M3-FP1

Body: AIR IN THE PUMP puts a bubble into the hypochlorite pump’s head. WEAK DELIVERY makes the next hypochlorite into the day tank weaker than it should be. Neither trips the plant; find each from the plant’s own displays.

Did you know?: A weak delivery sets off no alarm: the controller simply doses more, and the day tank empties faster.

Try it: Press a fault, then put it right. RESET SIMULATION clears both.
