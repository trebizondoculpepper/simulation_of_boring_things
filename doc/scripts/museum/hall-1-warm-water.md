## Entrance panel

Heading: Hall 1 · Warm water

Title: Warm water

Subtitle: Hall 1: heating, energy and a first pump

Introduction: A pool is a huge bath that is always going cold. Heating it is a chain: gas burns, hot water carries the heat, and an exchanger hands it to the pool without the waters ever mixing. To warm this pool by one degree takes 547 kWh: about 3,250 kettles. Everything here works: every valve, switch and dial is yours to try.

## Lectern 1: The gas

Headline: The gas

Title: Ideal gas main · M1-GS1

Body: Gas from the street, counted by the meter FQ1. The yellow lever GS1-V1 is the emergency control valve: one quarter-turn shuts off the whole building. The gauge PI3 shows the gas’s pressure, in millibars.

Did you know?: A cubic metre of natural gas holds about 10.6 kWh of energy.

Try it: Watch the meter’s red drums speed up when the boiler fires.

## Lectern 2: The boiler

Headline: The boiler

Title: Condensing boiler · M1-B1

Body: A flame heats water to about 73 °C, and the flue takes the burnt gases up through the roof. The boiler will not fire unless the pool’s water is flowing, and if its flame fails it locks itself out until someone presses RESET on the boiler itself.

Did you know?: A condensing boiler is about 95 % efficient: it even recovers the heat in the steam of its own flue gases.

Try it: Shut the gas cock B1-V1 while the boiler fires: watch it lock out. Open the cock and press RESET on the boiler.

## Lectern 3: The exchanger

Headline: The exchanger

Title: Plate heat exchanger · M1-HX1

Body: Two waters, one hot and one cool, pass each other through thin steel plates. The heat crosses; the water never does. The boiler’s water goes round its own sealed circuit, pumped by the twin-head pump P1.

Did you know?: The boiler’s water leaves about 17 degrees cooler; the pool’s only 0.6 of a degree warmer, because there is so much more of it.

Try it: Compare TI3 and TI4 with TI1 and TI2.

## Lectern 4: The pump

Headline: The pump

Title: Pool pump · M1-P2

Body: Water does not move by itself. The pump pushes it round, 185 m³ an hour; the gauges either side show the pressure it adds, and the flow switch FS1 tells the boiler the water is moving.

Did you know?: Shut the pump’s valve and it can only churn the same water: it warms up, and its thermostat trips it to save it.

Try it: Close the valve P2-V2 notch by notch: the flow FT1 falls and the pressure PI2 rises.

## Lectern 5: The pool

Headline: The pool

Title: Ideal pool · M1-IP1

Body: The thermostat TC1 calls for heat until the pool reaches its setting (28 °C to start; it is 27 °C now). ON-OFF: the boiler fires flat out, then stops, and the pool saw-tooths round the setting. MODULATING: the boiler turns itself down as the pool nears it, and the temperature settles.

Did you know?: To warm this 470 m³ pool by one degree takes 547 kWh: about 3,250 kettles.

Try it: Turn the thermostat’s dial, and switch its CONTROL between ON-OFF and MODULATING.

## Lectern 6: Where the heat goes

Headline: Where the heat goes

Title: The pool’s heat balance · M1-QI1 to M1-QI4

Body: Most of a pool’s heat leaves from its surface, as water evaporates: about 21 kW of the 23 kW this pool loses at 28 °C. A little goes into the ground; a warm hall gives a little back. (These meters are the museum’s model of the heat balance: no real plant measures them. Heat lost as radiation is left out.)

Did you know?: Busy swimmers splash up roughly 2× the evaporation of a calm pool.

Try it: Turn the SWIMMERS dial and watch the evaporation needle move.

## Lectern 7: The gas it burns

Headline: The gas it burns

Title: Gas burnt now · M1-EC1

Body: Every kWh of heat in the pool came from gas the meter counted. This display shows the gas the boiler is burning right now, in cubic metres an hour and as kilowatts of heat going in; watch it drop as the boiler turns itself down, or stop when it cycles off. The heat meter HM1 counts what reached the pool; the difference is the boiler’s loss.

Did you know?: Holding this pool at 28 °C takes about 591 kWh of gas a day, just to stand still.

Try it: Press TIME ×3600, turn the SWIMMERS dial down, and watch the gas fall as the pool needs less.
