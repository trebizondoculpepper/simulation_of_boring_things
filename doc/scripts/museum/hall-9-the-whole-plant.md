## Lectern 1: The desk

Headline: The desk

Title: Control desk · M9-UA1, M9-QI1

Body: The mimic shows everything. The annunciator tells you something is wrong. But most things are put right at the machine: the desk only starts and stops the pumps and opens and shuts the heating valve HX1-V2. Resets, selectors and hand valves are out in the plant.

Did you know?: A real pool plant room is often looked after by one person on a shift.

Try it: Press LAMP TEST: every window lights. Then read the water panel: chlorine, pH, temperature and flow, each from its own instrument.

## Lectern 2: The water

Headline: The water

Title: Circulation · M9-P1, M9-F1

Body: The pool overflows to its balance tank; the pump draws it through its basket strainer ST1, pushes it through the sand filter and the heat exchanger, and back to the pool. You met the pump in Hall 1 and the filter in Hall 2.

Did you know?: The strainer's basket catches hair and plasters before they reach the pump. When it blocks, the suction gauge PI1 goes below zero.

Try it: Turn the pump's selector to OFF, shut P1-V1 and P1-V2, and lift the strainer's lid.

## Lectern 3: The heat

Headline: The heat

Title: Heating · M9-B1, M9-HX1

Body: The boiler heats its own water; the pump P2 carries it to the exchanger, where the thermostat TC1 opens the motorised valve HX1-V2 when the pool wants heat. You met all of it in Hall 1.

Did you know?: The pool holds so much heat that a heating failure takes hours to show on its thermometer. Watch the thermostat's CALL lamp and the two thermometers either side of the exchanger instead.

Try it: If TI1 and TI2 read the same while the thermostat calls, no heat is getting through.

## Lectern 4: The chemistry

Headline: The chemistry

Title: Dosing · M9-SC1, M9-DC1

Body: A trickle of pool water runs past the probes in the sample cell; the controller doses hypochlorite and acid to hold the chlorine and pH. You met the controller in Hall 3 and the chemistry in Hall 4.

Did you know?: Automatic control is checked, never trusted: staff test the water by hand several times a day, and calibrate the controller to the test.

Try it: Test the water with the kit TK1 and compare it with the desk's chlorine reading.

## Lectern 5: The fault button

Headline: The fault button

Title: Fault pedestal · M9-FP1

Body: Press it. Something will go wrong. Read the alarm, find it, fix it, reset. The scoreboard times you from the fault to your first ACCEPT, and to the plant put right.

Did you know?: The first window to light is usually nearest the cause: it flashes faster than the rest.

Try it: There are 10 faults, and the button never gives you the same one twice running.
