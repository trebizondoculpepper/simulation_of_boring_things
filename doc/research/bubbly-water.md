# Bubbly water for the jacuzzi

Research note, 2026-10-21. The question: how to make the jacuzzi's water (`buildJacuzzi` in `src/toolkit/gallery/rooms/plant/pool-amenities.ts`) look aerated and churning while its jets and blower run, and calm and clear when they are off, cheaply, in Three.js 0.186, in keeping with the lo-fi look and the post chain (GTAO, bloom, film grain).

## What exists now

The tub's water is a 0.001 m `CylinderGeometry` disc (radius 1.4 m, out over the gutter, 72 segments, no interior vertices) with a transparent `MeshPhysicalMaterial` (`#83d7d9`, opacity 0.5, `depthWrite: false`, `renderOrder = 1`). `rippleWater` (`src/toolkit/gallery/views/round-room-light.ts`) gives it two scrolling ripple normal maps (normal map plus clearcoat normal map). Running is shown by raising `normalScale` (0.7 to 3.5), 300 instanced spheres rising from the seat edge (r ≈ 1.12 m) and the floor ring (r ≈ 0.8 m), and eight translucent cones in front of the wall jets. The spa state (`src/toolkit/components/pool/logic/spa-controller.ts`) gives `jets` and `air` as seconds left in each cycle, so they are independent on/off signals. The big pools use a separate `ShaderMaterial` (`createPoolWater` in `src/toolkit/components/pool/views/pool-surfaces.ts`) with analytic ripples (`POOL_RIPPLE_GLSL`), a room cube map and Schlick Fresnel.

## What real aerated spa water looks like

Water full of small bubbles turns white and opaque because each bubble surface reflects and refracts light in all directions, so the mixture scatters all wavelengths evenly, as in a waterfall or a breaking wave ([Science ABC](https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/why-does-water-appear-white-while-going-over-a-waterfall), [Surfertoday](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/why-do-breaking-waves-turn-white)). In a running hot tub this gives a recognisable pattern:

- Over the blower's air ring (seat edge, floor ring) the surface is a band of white, boiling water: rounded mounds a few centimetres high that bulge up and collapse continuously, with a little spray of droplets a few centimetres above them. The tub floor and seat cannot be seen through it.
- Over each hydrotherapy jet (venturi jets entrain air) there is a pale plume under water heading out from the wall and a turbulent, lumpy boil on the surface some 20 to 40 cm out from the wall; between jets and in the middle the water is agitated but clearer.
- Foam patches drift outward from the boils and, in a deck-level overflow tub such as ours, towards the gutter grating, where they pile up a little. The water level rises slightly when the air runs (the bubbles take up volume), so more spills into the gutter.
- When the blower stops the whiteness clears in a few seconds as the bubbles rise; the jets' turbulence dies away a little faster. The water goes back to clear, gently rippling and slightly blue.

## Techniques surveyed

- **Procedural foam in the water shader.** Cellular (Worley/Voronoi) noise, thresholded and animated, is the usual cheap foam texture: the distance to the nearest feature point gives round cells whose borders read as bubble clusters ([The Book of Shaders, ch. 12](https://thebookofshaders.com/12/), [Quilez, voronoise](https://iquilezles.org/articles/voronoise/)). Stylised water shaders mask such noise by depth difference or another mask to place foam ([Godot toon water](https://godotshaders.com/shader/toon-style-3d-water-shader-no-textures-needed/), [three.js forum, water with foam](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/unlit-water-shader-with-foam/11641), [Envato Tuts+, toon water part 2](https://code.tutsplus.com/creating-toon-water-for-the-web-part-2--cms-30485t)). For us the mask is analytic: we know exactly where the air holes and jets are.
- **Flow maps.** Advecting a texture along a vector field with two phase-offset copies crossfaded to hide the reset, as Valve did in Left 4 Dead 2 and Portal 2 ([Vlachos, SIGGRAPH 2010](<https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf>), [GameDev.net, foam with flow mapping](https://gamedev.net/shaderlab/53-foam-with-flow-mapping/)). `createPoolWater` already does exactly this under `USE_FLOW`. For a round tub the flow is simple and analytic (radially outward from the boils, a slow swirl from the angled jets), so no flow texture is needed.
- **Vertex displacement.** A subdivided disc displaced by a few centimetres over the boils gives real silhouette bumps and moving highlights. Cheap at a few thousand vertices; needs recomputed normals in the shader (finite differences of the same height function).
- **Opacity and colour from aeration.** Raising opacity and lerping the body colour toward a pale blue-white where aerated is the single biggest cue, and it is nearly free. It also hides the lack of refraction: aerated water should not show the floor anyway.
- **Particles, sprites and decals.** Billboard foam sprites or a few spray points over the boils add sparkle; a decal disc at each boil is an alternative to shader masking. Particles need transparency sorting care, and with the bubbles already instanced, more particles cost more than shader maths.
- **Screen-space refraction and depth foam.** Sampling the scene behind the water (transmission or a copied colour buffer) and depth-intersection foam need extra render targets and are costly in a software renderer; the effect is mostly hidden by aeration anyway. Not worth it here.
- **Shipped games.** Big titles build the ocean from an FFT simulation and derive foam from wave-peak masks and scattering approximations ([Sea of Thieves, SIGGRAPH 2018](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)), and real-time volumetric foam has been done with particles and depth maps ([Bagar et al. 2010](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2010/bagar2010/)). These are overkill for a 2.4 m tub. Small pools and fountains in games are almost always a flat or lightly displaced surface with scrolling or flowing foam textures, a few foam/spray sprite cards over the source, and opacity masks. That is the approach below.

## Recommendation

### 1. Aeration in the water material (do this first)

Keep the existing `MeshPhysicalMaterial` and its ripple normal maps, so the calm tub matches every other `rippleWater` surface, and patch it with `onBeforeCompile` to add foam. (Alternatively, give the jacuzzi a variant of `createPoolWater` with a `USE_FOAM` define; that matches the big pools' look instead and is easier to reason about, but loses the physical lighting.)

- **Uniforms:** `time`; `jets` and `air`, each 0..1, eased in the room's `update` from the spa state (rise over about 1.5 s, fall over about 4 s for air so foam lingers, 2 s for jets); `tubCentre`; `airRadii` (vec2: 0.8 and 1.12 m); `jetAngles` (the eight angles, or computed from an index in the shader as `(i + 0.5) / 8 * 2π`).
- **Masks, in the tub's polar coordinates:** `airMask` is a soft band (smoothstep, half-width about 0.08 m) at each air radius, broken up by low-frequency noise so it is a chain of boils, not a perfect ring; `jetMask` is the sum of eight soft blobs about 0.3 m out from the wall at the jet angles, stretched radially. `aeration = clamp(air * airMask + 0.6 * jets * jetMask + 0.15 * max(air, jets), 0, 1)`; the last term gives general cloudiness everywhere while anything runs.
- **Foam pattern:** two octaves of animated Worley noise (3×3 cell search each), scrolled radially outward from the boils (`p - normalize(p) * t * 0.1`) with the two-phase flow crossfade, thresholded with `smoothstep(0.35 - aeration * 0.3, 0.5, cells)` so foam grows denser as aeration rises. A slight swirl term gives the jets' circulation. Quantise or keep the noise coarse (cells of 3 to 6 cm) to suit the PS1 look.
- **Shading:** in the fragment, after `<color_fragment>`, `diffuseColor.rgb = mix(diffuseColor.rgb, vec3(0.86, 0.94, 0.95), 0.6 * aeration + 0.4 * foam)` and `diffuseColor.a = mix(diffuseColor.a, 0.95, max(aeration, foam))`; roughness up toward 0.5 in foam so it is matte and does not catch sharp highlights; scale the normal perturbation by `1 + 3 * aeration` locally (instead of the present global `normalScale` bump, which makes the calm parts too rough). Keep the foam albedo below the bloom threshold so the tub does not glow.
- **Geometry, for bulges:** replace the 0.001 m cylinder with a flat polar grid (e.g. `RingGeometry(0.001, outer, 96, 64)` rotated flat: 96 segments × 64 rings, about 6,000 vertices; `CircleGeometry` has no interior vertices to displace). In the vertex shader (`<begin_vertex>`), lift `transformed.y` by `0.03 * air * airMask * boil(p, t) + 0.015 * jets * jetMask * boil(...)`, where `boil` is a cheap sum of sines or value noise that pulses each mound up and down at 1 to 3 Hz; add a uniform `0.01 * air` lift of the whole surface for the displaced volume. Compute the normal from finite differences of the same function. Keep the rim vertices fixed so the surface still meets the gutter kerb.

Cost: one extra full-surface pass of maths over a disc that covers a modest part of the screen; two Worley octaves are 18 hash-and-distance evaluations per pixel, trivial on a GPU and acceptable in SwiftShader at this coverage. No new render targets, no extra draw calls.

### 2. A few spray and foam sprites (optional garnish)

Over the air ring, reuse the bubbles' pattern: a small `InstancedMesh` or `Points` (50 to 100) of white droplets that pop up 2 to 6 cm above the surface at random boil positions and fall back, visible only while `air > 0`. Drawn alpha-tested (not blended) to dodge sorting, they sparkle under the skylight and sell the boiling. The existing rising bubbles can then be reduced in number, since the aerated surface hides most of them; keep those just below the surface so they read through the gaps in the foam.

### Pitfalls

- **Transparency order.** The water is blended with `depthWrite: false` and `renderOrder = 1`. The rising bubbles and jet cones are transparent too: give them `renderOrder = 0` so they draw before the water, and spray above the surface `renderOrder = 2`, or they will flicker in and out as the camera moves. Alpha-tested particles avoid the problem entirely.
- **GTAO and depth.** With `depthWrite: false` the surface is invisible to GTAO, so the opaque-looking foam will show ambient occlusion from the tub floor beneath it. If it shows, write depth where `aeration` is high (a second, depth-only pass of the same mesh, or alpha-to-coverage), or accept it.
- **Software rendering.** Fragment cost scales with the pixels covered, so keep the noise to two octaves and avoid `pow`-heavy lighting additions; vertex displacement on 6,000 vertices is cheap. Avoid `transmission` on the physical material, which adds a whole extra scene render.
- **Matching the ripple look.** The calm state must be pixel-identical to today: make every new term multiply by `jets`, `air` or `aeration`, so at zero the shader reduces to the present one. Drive the shader from eased 0..1 values, never directly from the spa's on/off, or the foam will snap.
- **Film grain and banding.** Large, flat, pale foam areas show banding after tone mapping; the existing film grain hides most of it, and the Worley texture breaks up the rest.
- **Shader patching.** `onBeforeCompile` string replacement depends on Three.js chunk names (`<begin_vertex>`, `<color_fragment>`, `<normal_fragment_maps>`); pin it with a comment naming the Three.js version (0.186.1) and set `customProgramCacheKey` so other physical materials do not share the patched program.
