# The poolrooms look: what makes it liminal, and how to render it

Research for the public spaces' visual treatment (the brief: "stark pale tiles, geometric repetition, concealed services, diffuse light, water and acoustic reverberation"). Written 2026-10-20.

## What the look is

The "poolrooms" are a strand of the liminal-space aesthetic: indoor pools and tiled halls that feel familiar, deserted and slightly wrong. The clearest reference is Jared Pike's _Dream Pools_ (Blender renders, after Alain Capeillères' 1970s pool designs): white tiles on every surface, clean geometric and curved forms, turquoise water, soft light lying across the water, and pools that run off into darkness ([gessato](https://www.gessato.com/dream-pools-artist-jared-pike/), [ignant](https://ignant.com/2022/02/24/watch-jared-pikes-soothing-oasis-dream-pools-ripple-against-geometric-white-tiled-designs/), [hypeandhyper](https://hypeandhyper.com/the-liminal-cosmos-of-jared-pike-dive/)). The game _POOLS_ builds on the same look: sterile white tiles, light shimmering off them, dim tiled corridors between bright halls ([fingerguns](https://fingerguns.net/games/2024/05/21/pools-review-pc-the-backroom-buoys/)).

Its ingredients, roughly in order of how much they matter:

1. **Light that seems to come from everywhere.** Overcast daylight from rooflights and high windows, bounced off white tile until there are almost no shadows, only darkening in corners and under edges. Whites are slightly over-exposed and glow.
2. **Tile on everything.** Small square tiles (100–150 mm) on floors, walls, pool shells, steps, ceilings and columns, with crisp grout lines that make the scale readable and the repetition endless. Coved, rounded junctions where wall meets floor.
3. **Water.** Shallow, clear, cyan, almost still; caustic light patterns rippling on the pool floor and nearby walls; reflections of the room in the surface.
4. **Space.** Symmetry, repetition and long sight lines; openings onto more of the same; no people, furniture or clutter; ambiguous scale; spaces that end in darkness.
5. **Atmosphere.** A faint haze that pales the distance; a narrow palette of white, cyan and teal; reverberant sound of lapping water and a distant hum.

What breaks it: visible light fittings and hard shadows, clutter, signage, dirt, anything that dates the space or explains it. That is in tension with this game: the public spaces are a real leisure centre with real (and legally required) safety signage and equipment. The fire safety fittings now in the pool hall will be one of the things to judge in a test (see below): fewer, cleaner, flush fittings help.

## How to get it in three.js

Everything below exists in the installed three.js (0.186).

| Ingredient                                                 | Technique                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Where in three.js                                                                                             | Cost                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Diffuse, shadowless bounce light                           | Baked lighting for the static architecture: either baked offline (Blender Cycles from a glTF export of the procedural rooms) and loaded as light maps, or accumulated in the browser with the progressive light map | `examples/jsm/misc/ProgressiveLightMap.js` (WebGL), `ProgressiveLightMapGPU.js` (WebGPU); `material.lightMap` | Baked: almost free at run time. The single biggest win.                     |
| Bounce light that moves (opening doors, changing daylight) | Screen-space global illumination on the WebGPU renderer                                                                                                                                                             | `examples/jsm/tsl/display/SSGINode.js`                                                                        | Expensive; test at reduced resolution.                                      |
| Darkened corners and edges                                 | Ambient occlusion, baked into the light map, or screen-space                                                                                                                                                        | `GTAONode.js` (WebGPU), `GTAOPass.js` (WebGL)                                                                 | Moderate.                                                                   |
| Glowing over-exposed whites                                | Filmic tone mapping (AgX) with exposure pushed up, and a soft wide bloom                                                                                                                                            | `renderer.toneMapping`, `BloomNode.js` / `UnrealBloomPass.js`                                                 | Cheap.                                                                      |
| Tiles                                                      | Generated tile textures with per-tile tint variation, a normal map for the grout, roughness that varies tile to tile so highlights break up                                                                         | Canvas or baked textures; `normalMap`, `roughnessMap`                                                         | Cheap.                                                                      |
| Water surface                                              | Reflection and refraction with flowing normal maps                                                                                                                                                                  | `examples/jsm/objects/Water2.js`                                                                              | A second render of the scene per water surface; keep to one or two at once. |
| Caustics                                                   | A tiling animated caustic texture projected in world space onto everything below and near the water line, injected into the tile materials' shaders                                                                 | Custom shader chunk (`onBeforeCompile`) or a TSL node                                                         | Cheap, and the second biggest win after lighting.                           |
| Room reflected in glossy tile                              | Screen-space reflections on the floor and wet surfaces, or a prefiltered environment captured from the room itself                                                                                                  | `SSRNode.js`; `CubeCamera` + `PMREMGenerator`                                                                 | SSR is expensive; a captured environment is cheap.                          |
| Light shafts from rooflights                               | God rays, or additive light cones                                                                                                                                                                                   | `GodraysNode.js`                                                                                              | Moderate.                                                                   |
| Haze                                                       | Exponential fog in a pale cyan-white                                                                                                                                                                                | `scene.fog = new FogExp2(...)`                                                                                | Free.                                                                       |
| Camera feel                                                | Slight vignette, film grain, a touch of chromatic aberration; a wide field of view                                                                                                                                  | `FilmNode.js`, `ChromaticAberrationNode.js`                                                                   | Cheap.                                                                      |
| Sound                                                      | A convolution reverb with a large tiled-hall impulse response, water lapping, a distant mechanical hum (the plant behind the walls)                                                                                 | Web Audio `ConvolverNode`; the toolkit's machine tones (dev7)                                                 | Cheap.                                                                      |

The Encabulator's Hall already draws with `WebGPURenderer` and a node pipeline (`src/rendering/node-pipeline.ts` there, not yet copied), which is the route to the WebGPU passes above.

## Recommended next step

The brief asks for an early visual feasibility scene: two tiled spaces, a doorway, water and representative lighting, measured before the environment is expanded. A development room for exactly that, with each ingredient switchable from a console so they can be compared and timed one at a time:

1. Baseline: the current materials and lights.
2. Tile materials: small tiles, grout normal map, varied roughness and tint.
3. Baked light: progressive light map with ambient occlusion.
4. Tone mapping, exposure and bloom.
5. Caustics.
6. Water reflection and refraction.
7. Haze, film grain and vignette.
8. WebGPU screen-space GI and reflections, if the baked version is not enough.

Record the frame time and draw calls of each on stated hardware, then carry the winning recipe into the leisure centre's public rooms.

## What dev11 found

dev11 (`src/toolkit/gallery/rooms/pool-room.ts`, surfaces in `src/toolkit/components/pool/views/pool-surfaces.ts`) is the feasibility room: a wading pool with steps, a channel through an arcade into a dim back hall, columns, coffered rooflights and a frosted east wall. Measured on 2026-10-20 in Chrome on the owner's Mac (1400 × 673 window), frame times averaged over a few seconds:

- **What made the look:** glazed tile drawn in the shader (world-projected, so every surface tiles at 125 mm without unwrapping, grout fading to its average colour with distance), the water's depth tint and caustics under it, softer caustics reflected on walls and ceiling near it, ambient occlusion, a captured "bounce" environment, AgX tone mapping, bloom on the rooflights, a light haze, and a faint vignette and grain.
- **Overexposure and haze:** the first versions were nearly white and milky. The causes were the captured bounce light (it also tinted the room green, because the capture saw the cyan pool: it is now captured with the water's tint and caustics switched off), bloom catching the white walls, AgX tone mapping greying the whites, and the haze. Now: ACES tone mapping at exposure 1.0, bloom only above the light sources' brightness, bounce 0.5, one area light over the rooflights and a weaker one at the glazing, and haze off (it is still a switch, `fog`).
- **Cost:** at the full Retina pixel ratio (2) the first version took 59 ms a frame. Ambient occlusion was most of it (about 13 ms), and drawing it at half resolution looks the same. A real mirror reflection of the room in the water (a Reflector, drawing the room again each frame) cost about 2 ms; it is replaced by a reflection looked up in a picture of the room taken once at load, which looks the same in a room in which nothing moves. Three area lights are merged into one. The glaze's clear coat costs about 1.5 ms. With all of that the room takes 18 ms a frame at pixel ratio 2, and it draws at 1.5 by default (`?dpr=2` to measure at full resolution).
- **Switches:** in the console, `poolRoom.ingredients` lists them and `poolRoom.set('ao', false)` switches one off; `poolRoom.stats()` gives the frame time.

## Published poolrooms projects

Searched 2026-10-20. Nobody has published a definitive shader recipe, but several open-source three.js poolrooms exist. None that were checked states a licence, so they are references to learn from, not code to copy.

- [ZhUyU1997/poolrooms](https://github.com/ZhUyU1997/poolrooms) (three.js, Vite) documents its settings: ACES tone mapping at exposure 0.95; a sun at intensity 9 through high windows with a 4096 px shadow map and volumetric light shafts with floating dust; image-based light at only 0.18 for indoor fill; a hemisphere light at 0.85 to fake bounce; a world-space "GI field" injected into materials with `onBeforeCompile`; procedural PBR textures; water with planar reflection at half resolution, refraction at 0.6, absorption, caustics and an underwater pass; then GTAO, bloom, colour grading, SMAA and an output pass. It aims for 60 fps at 1080p on a laptop RTX 3060, and as the frame rate falls it degrades in order: resolution (down to 0.62), then GTAO off, then weaker bloom.
- [bkhan23544/poolrooms-threejs](https://github.com/bkhan23544/poolrooms-threejs) (three.js WebGPU/TSL; [forum post](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/made-a-poolrooms-liminal-space-scene-with-three-js-webgpu/95372)): tiles generated in the shader from world-space coordinates with antialiased grout and colour variation (as dev11 does); layered directional waves for the water's normals with Fresnel; planar reflections on floor, water, walls and ceiling at different resolutions, skipped when out of view; bloom, a fisheye "bodycam" distortion, slight chromatic aberration and film grain; a 1K studio HDR for environment light; distance fog.
- [ZEQUWEN/poolrooms-horror](https://github.com/ZEQUWEN/poolrooms-horror) and [ronpicard/pool-rooms-game-web-app](https://github.com/ronpicard/pool-rooms-game-web-app) are browser games in three.js and Vite; [rfsheffer/PoolRooms](https://github.com/rfsheffer/PoolRooms) is an MIT-licensed Unity interior for Lethal Company.
- Paid Blender courses (for example [Poolrooms in Blender](https://superhivemarket.com/products/poolrooms-backrooms-in-blender-step-by-step-tutorial-beginner-friendly-how-to-create)) cover tile imperfections built as real geometry, an ocean-modifier water surface, caustics, daylight and VHS-style post-processing.

What they agree on, against dev11: ACES at about 0.95 exposure (dev11 now uses ACES at 1.0); low image-based fill with a hemisphere light standing in for bounce; procedural world-space tiles; Fresnel water from layered waves. What dev11 lacks that the most complete of them has: **direct sunlight through high windows, with real shadows and light shafts**. Patches of sun on white tile are what give their scenes contrast; dev11's light is all soft, which is part of why it read as flat and washed out.

## Three looks on one lectern

dev11 now has a lectern by its door, after the Encabulator's fires room: a LOOK selector with three positions and six knobs (exposure, light, bounce, bloom, caustics, bump), each at its middle leaving the look as given and turning from nothing to double (exposure two stops either way). The looks are defined in `src/toolkit/gallery/logic/pool-looks.ts`; `?look=sunlight` starts in one, and in the console `poolRoom.look('unity')` and `poolRoom.knob('bloom', 0.8)` hold one.

- **Overcast:** soft light from the rooflights and glazing as area lights, bounced by the captured room.
- **Sunlight:** after ZhUyU1997/poolrooms: a sun through the rooflights at about 57° casting 2048 px shadows, additive light shafts, a hemisphere light at 0.85 standing in for bounce, and image light at 0.18; ACES at 0.95.
- **Unity:** values read from the Unity HDRP project [rfsheffer/PoolRooms](https://github.com/rfsheffer/PoolRooms) (MIT): its tile, grout and ceiling materials are HDRP Lit at smoothness 0.5 (so satin, roughness about 0.5, no clear coat); its volume profile has bloom intensity 0.2 with scatter 0.7, ambient occlusion 0.5 over 1.5 m, ACES tone mapping and automatic exposure; its pool room's lamps are 933 lm spots at 20 000 K with a 51.7 % inner cone. Twelve cool spot lamps stand in for them. Unity's bloom is blended rather than added, so a threshold of 1.2 keeps the white walls from glowing.

Every look's tiles are now set a little off true, each at its own slight tilt with a faintly wavy glaze, and the clear coat follows the bump, so highlights break up tile by tile.

## What the artists and developers say

Searched 2026-10-20 (Reddit could not be reached by the search tools; no numeric material recipes were found anywhere).

- **Light:** Jared Pike says the look that defined _Dream Pools_ came from "warm sunlight, especially when juxtaposed with unlit corners"; he exposes part of a scene in sun and then deliberately makes a dark area, and the light's source is often unclear ([designboom](https://www.designboom.com/art/diving-3d-artist-jared-pike-imaginary-liminal-interiors-pool-dreamscapes-02-18-2024/), [Scare Me](https://scare-me.beehiiv.com/p/interview-artist-jared-pike-on-dream-pools-and-the-allure-of-liminal-spaces), [Northeastern](https://news.northeastern.edu/2025/06/17/dream-pools-viral-jared-pike/)). He renders in LuxCoreRender and finishes in Lightroom ([PLAIN](https://plainmagazine.com/jared-pike-dream-pools-3d-artworks/)).
- **POOLS (Tensori, Unity):** baked lighting, light probes and adaptive probe volumes, reflection probes, bloom and tone mapping; screen-space GI is its most demanding setting ([itch.io](https://tensori.itch.io/pools), [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2663530/discussions/0/6361972321502338713/)). Players criticised light leaking round pillars, areas lit that should be dark, and weak ambient occlusion. Tensori keep the water moving: still water "just doesn't look good".
- **Camera:** Pike uses a 24 or 35 mm lens at eye or waist height, framed where a person could have taken the photo, and says "it's really important that you can't see everything". (dev11's 68° vertical field of view is about a 24 mm lens.)
- **Clean versus real:** Pike keeps everything clean and perfect but "just enough to feel realistic"; others warn that renders fail when too clean or plastic, and courses teach adding imperfections ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/blender-artist-uses-camera-tricks-to-create-a-liminal-dreamcore-scene)). A perfectly level, still camera reads as fake ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/liminal-hallway/1590592)).

For dev11 this argues for the sunlight look with much less fill (warm sun, corners that fall dark), and against lighting the whole room evenly.
