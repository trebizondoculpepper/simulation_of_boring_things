# Services through the corridors

Stage 1 of prompt 088 (`doc/prompts/088-corridor-services.md`), researched 2026-10-27. It covers every service the game's model sends from one room to another, what each one is and how big it is, which are ring mains and which run point to point, and where each runs in the corridors, shelf by shelf. The layer it feeds is decoration only: no valves, no state, nothing bound to the plant model. The building is a fantasy and is not one consistent three-dimensional space, so positions are approximate. **Guesses are marked (guess).** Anything without that mark is taken from the code, with a file:line citation.

## Frame and conventions

`src/game/facility-plan.ts`, which the mission and the README name, no longer exists. The plant's corridor is now the **main corridor**, a square ring in `src/toolkit/gallery/logic/main-corridor.ts`. Its outside is 28 m a side, it is 4 m wide and 7 m tall, and its frame puts the north-west outside corner at the origin, with x east, z south and the floor at y = 0 (main-corridor.ts:3-25). Every other main-plant room opens off its outside walls. The wet hall fills the 20 m block in its middle (main-corridor.ts:63-91; shell-rooms.ts:366-369). The corridor has a generated services ceiling (main-corridor.ts:6-7), and its zone starts 1.9 m below the 7 m roof (room-structure.ts:56), so there is open space from the floor up to **5.1 m**.

Heights in this document are above the corridor floor. The plant rooms' plant floors are at corridor level, because their equipment doors open onto the corridor at plant-floor level (shell-plan.ts:69-70; main-corridor.ts:4). The wet hall's floor is 1.75 m lower, half a storey down (shell-rooms.ts:369). So **a height in the wet hall's frame minus 1.75 m gives the corridor height**. The wet hall's frame is centred, so its point (x, z) is the ring's point (x + 14, z + 14).

## Rings and point-to-point services

The owner, 2026-10-27 (088, "Ring mains"): the ring corridor carries ring mains. A service is a ring where many rooms round the corridor each take a share of something sent out from one place. A service runs point to point where it is one room's own line, where it flows only toward one place, or where safety forbids a ring.

**How the rings are sized.**

- **LTHW ring: DN100 (114.3 mm OD steel), lagged.** It has to feed its two largest branches, which are 114.3 mm each: PL1 (room-pipes.ts:915-916) and PL2 (olympic-plumbing.ts:855-868). At 1 to 1.5 m/s, a 114.3 mm pipe (about 107 mm bore) carries 32 to 49 m³/h, which is about 0.75 to 1.1 MW at the 20 K drop the sizing uses (sizing.ts:122-124). The site is "about 1 MW of boilers" (utility-meters.ts:38), with 300 kW boilers (sizing.ts:106-107), six of them (boiler-house-schematic.ts:28-31). The boiler house's own building main is only 88.9 mm (room-pipes.ts:1407, 1577-1592), which caps it near 0.45 to 0.67 MW. That is a discrepancy (below). Lagging is 40 mm with a metal or PVC jacket (guess), so about 0.19 m over the cladding.
- **Make-up ring: 76.1 mm copper**, the booster set's discharge (wet-hall-plumbing.ts:191-192). At 1.5 m/s it carries about 22 m³/h, plenty for branches of 54, 54, 50 and 32 mm.
- **Power ladder: 450 mm wide heavy-duty cable ladder, 100 mm rails** (guess). The switchboard's eight ways add up to 1,058 A against a 1,600 A incomer (electrical-switchgear.ts:394-411). Their SWA cables, at about 26 to 48 mm each (guess, from usual BS 7671 sizes), plus lighting, emergency lighting and the generator cable, fit in one layer on 450 mm.
- **Control tray: 300 mm perforated tray** (guess), at least 300 mm from the power ladder.

## How the shelves are arranged

Two racks run the full length of every segment. Electrical and control services go on the **inner wall** (the wet hall side), which keeps them apart from the wet services. Wet services go on the **outer wall**, because that is where most of the tee-offs are: the plant rooms and the boiler house are all on the outer wall. Services leaving or entering the wet hall cross the corridor overhead between the racks. Power drops to the outer rooms cross at 4.9 m, above every wet service, so **water never runs above electrics**.

## Discrepancies found in the model

These are reported, not fixed. Stage 1 changes no code.

1. **The backwash leaves PL1 and PL2 by floor channel** (olympic-plumbing.ts:65-66, 590-594; room-pipes.ts:568-610). But the wet hall takes it as a **160 mm main high in its north wall** (wet-hall-plumbing.ts:174-182; shell-rooms.ts:418-423). A gravity channel at the floor cannot climb to 2.45 m. PL2's channel is also DN300, sized for about 210 m³/h (olympic-plumbing.ts:268-269), and a 160 mm main carrying that would run at about 3.5 m/s.
2. **The chemical branches are 32 mm at the wet hall and at PL2**, but **25 mm at PL1, PL3 and PL4** (wet-hall-plumbing.ts:165-168; olympic-plumbing.ts:660; day-tank-plumbing.ts:67).
3. **The boiler house's building flow and return are 88.9 mm** (room-pipes.ts:1407, 1580, 1650). PL1's and PL2's heat exchanger primaries are 114.3 mm (room-pipes.ts:916; olympic-plumbing.ts:858, 865), larger than their source.
4. **The calorifier's cold feed has no source.** It is a `water-service` boundary (boiler-house-schematic.ts:105, 202-207), and the only water the model brings into the building goes into the Category 5 break tank.
5. **The air plant takes heat and water in `services.ts:15`**, but its schematic has no heating or water links (back-room-schematics.ts:180-203).
6. **Make-up and hot water are drawn plain green in the rooms** (room-pipework.ts:69, 77). BS 1710, as `plant-tools.ts:91-105` lists it, gives drinking water a blue band and hot water supply a white band. The corridor layer can use the right bands, but it will then not match the rooms.
7. **PL2's busbar is fed through its east wall** (olympic-plumbing.ts:704-708), but its corridor wall is its south. The corridor can only reach it approximately, at the room's east end.
8. **`facility-plan.ts` no longer exists**, but the README (README.md:32) and older planning notes (`doc/planning/plant-interconnections.md`) still name it.

## The owner's rulings

- **Levels between rooms (2026-10-27):** the building's geography is not modelled consistently, so between rooms levels and gravity do not matter. A line that goes down into a gully or a floor channel in one room may emerge at whatever level the next room needs: the backwash leaves the plant rooms by their floor channels and arrives in the wet hall at its high stub, and the corridor shows each service at the corridor's own height for it. Within a room, gravity lines must still fall. This settles question 1, and question 2 likewise: the wet hall's low stubs need no risers in the corridor.
