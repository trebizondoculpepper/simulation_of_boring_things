# Daylight details adapted from the architect drawings

The [Sport England six-lane-plus drawing set](https://sportengland-production-files.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/6-lane-pool-plus-pdf-set.pdf) gives a floor plan, sections and elevations for a plausible community pool. Sheet 1 identifies high-level windows (key 8), aluminium curtain walling (9), and rooflights to the pool hall (6). Sheet 2 labels low-rise barrel-vault rooflights (11), high-level windows (4), and sun tubes over changing (12). Sheet 3 repeats the high windows and rooflights on the elevations. These are the direct precedents for the hall's high windows and four shallow barrel rooflights. The changing room uses smaller high windows. Our dimensions and positions are adapted to the fictional 80 × 50 m hall; they are not traced from the drawing. The four rooflights occupy 18% of its ceiling area.

The [London Aquatics Centre Olympic-mode ground-floor drawing](https://cdn.archilovers.com/projects/ef0dbf8b-ba23-4ffe-b05b-46826f2dc1cb.pdf) informed the earlier public-space discussion, but it does not provide a rooflight schedule for this model. The present glazing follows the Sport England architectural sheets instead. [Sport England's swimming-pool design guidance](https://www.sportengland.org/media/4187/swimming-pools-dgn-2013.pdf) also discusses controlling roof daylight and glare; this prototype has no blinds or lighting controls yet.

The engineering-room finish adapts the visual language of `/Users/salimfadhley/workspace/encabulator/src/scene/dev-corridor.ts`: painted breeze block, sealed concrete, dark skirting, coloured route stripes, orange opening markers, fluorescent battens and high cable trays. The source corridor's loop geometry, doors and props were not copied because this facility has its own rectangular connected-room plan.
