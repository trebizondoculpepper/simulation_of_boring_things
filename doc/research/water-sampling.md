# Water sampling and water-quality monitors

Researched 2026-10-20 to answer the owner's question: can the plant have a sampling bypass (a sample line) feeding water-quality monitors, and which room would it be in? Short answer: yes, and it is not optional. Every public pool with automatic dosing has one: a small continuous sample of the circulating water runs through a flow cell holding the probes, and the controller on the same panel drives the dosing pumps from what the probes read. The game already has the panel (the "Controller and sample cells" exhibit in `dev_dosing`, `src/toolkit/gallery/rooms/plant/dosing.ts`) and a repeat of it in the control room. What it lacks is the sample line itself as part of the plant model.

Statements marked _(inferred)_ are my reasoning from the sources, not something a source states. Everything else carries its source.

## 1. How the sample is taken

**It is the standard arrangement.** PWTAG's Code of Practice says public pools should have instruments that monitor disinfectant residual and pH continuously, "normally linked to the chemical dosing system", and that automatic dosing is "essential for public pools" ([PWTAG Code of Practice, July 2021, §3.4 and §9.2](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). The probes sit in a "sample cell" fed by a sample line; the Code refers to "manual tests of the sample cell" for checking the controller (same, §9.2).

**Where it is taken from.** PWTAG Technical Note 49 says: "Commonly the sample point for the controller is after the circulation pump, relying on the pump for a steady flow" ([PWTAG TN49, July 2020](https://www.pwtag.org/pool-circulation-pumps-variable-speed-drives/)). The Code fixes its position relative to dosing indirectly: polyaluminium chloride coagulant "should be dosed as far upstream of the filters as possible, but after the chemical controller's sample point" ([PWTAG CoP §5.5](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). A maker's installation manual says the same and adds the general rule: the sample inlet "must be installed upstream of the flocculant dosing station. Otherwise, it could influence the measurements", it goes "in the pool return or as a pool extraction" according to local regulations, and it must give "a representative" sample with "a constant, bubble-free flow" ([Wallace & Tiernan DEPOLOX Pool Compact manual, §5.4.6](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)).

So the usual tapping is on the pump discharge main, between the circulation pumps and the filters, before any chemical goes in. Water there has just come back from the pool by way of the balance tank, so it shows what the pool holds _(inferred)_. That is the point of the location: the controller is meant to measure the pool, not the dose. Hypochlorite and acid are injected later, on the return after the filters (PWTAG puts pH correction "downstream from the filters – where possible, after the heat exchanger", and with UV or ozone the chlorine dose always comes after the secondary treatment, [CoP §12.12](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). If the sample were taken just downstream of an injection point, the probes would read freshly dosed water, high in chlorine or low in pH, and the controller would back off while the pool itself ran short _(inferred)_.

**How the sample is drawn.** Pump pressure does the work. The W&T flow cell needs 0.25 to 3.0 bar at its inlet and at least 0.25 bar more at the inlet than at the outlet; below 0.25 bar "a booster pump must be used", above 3.0 bar a pressure-reducing valve ([DEPOLOX Pool Compact manual §5.4.6](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)). TN49 notes that when variable-speed drives slow the circulation pumps, "less water flows through the sample cell", readings go wrong or the controller locks out on low flow, and "a separate pump can overcome the low flow problem" ([TN49](https://www.pwtag.org/pool-circulation-pumps-variable-speed-drives/)). The German standard's plant lists include a dedicated _Messwasserpumpe_ (sample-water pump) ([BVS pool plant check questions](https://www.bvs.de/fileadmin/mediapool/KontrollfragenBaeder.pdf)). The line itself is small: the W&T manual asks for 6 × 3 mm PVC hose or DN 6 tube, "as short as possible", because "long measuring dead times mean poorer control quality", with an optional 0.5 mm strainer at the inlet, isolating ball valves at inlet and outlet, a check valve and a pressure gauge on the tapping; copper pipe is forbidden as it distorts the readings (same manual, §5.4.6 to 5.4.9).

**Flow rate.** The W&T pool flow cell holds a controlled 33 l/h through a flow control valve; its flow monitor switches at 21 ± 3 l/h ([DEPOLOX Pool Compact brochure](https://trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/DEPOLOX-Pool-Compact-Brochure-2019.pdf), [MFC-DEPOLOX 5 data sheet](https://epiint.evoqua.com/siteassets/documents/products/a_temp_disimfection/wt.050.580.001.de.ps.mfc-depolox-5.pdf)). PWTAG TN39 notes that the older bare-electrode amperometric cells are "sensitive to flow and pressure variations", so the flow through the cell "is regulated by a flow control valve" ([PWTAG TN39, Residual control, Sept 2018](https://www.pwtag.org/residual-control-september-2018/)). A DPD colorimetric analyser takes a little more: the Hach CL17 needs 200 to 500 ml/min to its sample conditioning and 60 to 200 ml/min through the instrument ([Iowa DNR CL17 checklist](https://iowadnr.gov/media/5491/download), [Hach CL17sc listing](https://usabluebook.com/hach-cl17sc-chlorine-analyzer-with-pressure-regula-53224)). An on-line turbidimeter (Hach 1720E) takes 200 to 650 ml/min ([Hach 1720E specification](https://cdn.hach.com/7FYZVWYB/at/79swkqt43jb93m745z8jg/1720E_sc_LR_Turbidity_CSI_Specifications.pdf)). Against a pool circulation of hundreds of cubic metres an hour, the sample is negligible.

**Where it goes afterwards.** Reagent-free probes (amperometric chlorine, pH, redox) leave the water unchanged, so "sample can be added back to the supply or drain" ([Trility, Depolox 3 plus](https://trility.com.au/product/depolox-3-plus-chlorine-analyser/)). The W&T cell accepts up to 1.5 bar back-pressure at its outlet, which allows it to return into a lower-pressure part of the circuit ([manual §5.4.8](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)). In a pool plant the obvious low-pressure point is the balance tank, or the pump suction _(inferred: no source names the balance tank)_. A DPD analyser adds reagent to the sample, so its outflow goes to drain; a drinking-water example disposes of analyser sample "in a sink behind the measuring circuit" ([Krohne application report 396](https://dam.krohne.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/f_pdf/v1/do-application/application-report-396-measurement-of-free-chlorine-in-a-drinking-water-system-en-gb)). A sample tap on the cell lets the operator draw water for calibration tests (W&T "sample extraction unit", manual §3).

## 2. The instruments and what they drive

- **Free chlorine.** Amperometric cells: the older bare-electrode type with copper and platinum electrodes kept clean by grit or beads, sometimes needing buffer injection; the membrane type; and the potentiostatic three-electrode type, the most accurate in clean water with no zero drift ([TN39](https://www.pwtag.org/residual-control-september-2018/)). W&T's pool cell still uses quartz grit for "hydro mechanical cleaning of the free chlorine sensor" ([brochure](https://trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/DEPOLOX-Pool-Compact-Brochure-2019.pdf)). The grit-cleaned bare-electrode cell is the period-correct choice for 2000 _(inferred from TN39 calling it the "older design")_.
- **Total and combined chlorine.** PWTAG says plainly that "automatic control does not monitor combined chlorine", so chloramines are found by the staff's manual tests (DPD 1 for free, DPD 3 for total; combined is the difference) ([CoP §9.2](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). Some analysers can measure total chlorine (Depolox 3 plus measures "free or total (combined) chlorine", [Trility](https://trility.com.au/product/depolox-3-plus-chlorine-analyser/)), and a DPD colorimetric analyser can be set up for either. In a 2000 public pool, combined chlorine on line would be unusual _(inferred)_.
- **DPD colorimetric analysers.** These mix DPD reagent into a batch of sample every 2.5 minutes and read the magenta colour; reagents last about 30 days in normal use ([Hach CL17 brochure](https://cdn.hach.com/7FYZVWYB/at/m782tqz7nhh867zcqj7gsn3/LIT1626.pdf)). They are found in drinking water more than in pools.
- **pH.** Glass electrode in the same cell; calibrated with two buffers, normally pH 4 and pH 9.2, never one ([CoP §9.2](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)).
- **Redox (ORP).** Responds to "any oxidiser in the water", not chlorine alone, and is pH-sensitive; an inferred chlorine figure shown from a redox probe is "purely inferential" ([TN39](https://www.pwtag.org/residual-control-september-2018/)). With UV, chlorine should be controlled amperometrically, since "UV does not oxidise" ([CoP §6.7](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). Usually fitted as a check alongside the chlorine cell.
- **Temperature.** A Pt1000 in the cell, used for compensation ([brochure](https://trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/DEPOLOX-Pool-Compact-Brochure-2019.pdf), [TN39](https://www.pwtag.org/residual-control-september-2018/)).
- **Turbidity.** PWTAG's accepted maximum is 0.5 NTU ([CoP §5](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). An on-line turbidimeter on the filtrate is possible but I found no source saying it was usual in UK pools of the period; it is a good candidate for the game as an optional extra _(inferred)_.
- **Residual ozone (ozone plants only).** PWTAG lists ozone contact and deozonising after filtration ([CoP §6.8](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)); the Depolox 3 plus could be configured to measure ozone ([Trility](https://trility.com.au/product/depolox-3-plus-chlorine-analyser/)), so the same kind of analyser on a sample taken after the deozoniser can prove no ozone is going back to the pool _(inferred)_. The parallel note `doc/research/ozone-plant.md` covers this.

**Control.** The controller compares each reading with its set point and drives the dosing pumps proportionally: pulse frequency or stroke length for solenoid pumps, or a 0/4–20 mA output; the W&T pool controller has relay and analogue outputs for chlorine (or redox) and pH, plus alarm contacts ([brochure](https://trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/DEPOLOX-Pool-Compact-Brochure-2019.pdf)); Bayrol's commercial controllers vary "dosing pulse length" of peristaltic pumps proportionally ([poolsystem.pl](https://poolsystem.pl/en/pool-dosing-and-measuring-stations/9-dosing-station-for-pool-chemicals-poolrelax-3-chlorine-bayrol.html)). pH correction is by acid or by CO₂ ([CoP §6](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)); the dosing room already has both.

**Checking against manual tests.** The controller's readings are checked daily against manual tests on the sample cell; recalibrate pH if the difference is more than 0.2, and chlorine if it is more than 15% after a confirming test; every calibration is logged ([CoP §9.2](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). Pool water itself is still tested manually, from the pool at 100–300 mm depth, at the deep end away from the inlets, "not from the sampling cell", at least three times a day with automatic control instead of two-hourly without it (§9.2, §9.4). The chlorine cell is zeroed first "with no flow passing through the sensor holder cell", then spanned against a DPD 1 reading ([TN39](https://www.pwtag.org/residual-control-september-2018/)). This is the test shelf and log already in `dev_dosing`.

**Alarms and interlocks.** Dosing must stop on loss of sample flow: W&T's manual says never to bridge the flow input "even temporarily", because without it "there is a risk of uncontrolled dosing of chemicals"; after a settable delay (0 to 10 minutes) both chlorine and pH pumps stop ([manual §2.3.1, §3](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)). Other built-in stops are circulation pump failure, chemical tank empty (suction lance level), and dosing-time monitoring, which stops a pump that has run too long without effect ([brochure](https://trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/DEPOLOX-Pool-Compact-Brochure-2019.pdf)). PWTAG says dosing pumps should "shut themselves off if the circulation system fails (though automatic water quality monitors should remain in operation)" ([CoP §3.4](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). Lutz-Jesco's sample station has the same flow-switch cut-out ([impeller.net](https://impeller.net/magazin/small-but-nice-water-sampling-station-easypool-smart-02)). Measured-value alarms (chlorine high and low, pH high and low) come out as relay contacts.

## 4. Which room

PWTAG says "electrical control panels, chemical control units, ozone generators etc should be in clean, dry, well-ventilated areas away from chemical stores, and on raised concrete work plinths", while chemicals are stored "in separate, secure rooms" ([CoP §11](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)). Dosing equipment should be close to the flow and return pipework, "to avoid extended systems" (§12.12). Gas detection goes in the store and "the area from where chemicals are dosed", and alarms go to "a continuously staffed area or control centre", with telemetry to control rooms where appropriate ([PWTAG TN62, May 2022](https://www.pwtag.org/gas-monitoring-in-the-plantroom-tn62/)). The sample line must be short ([W&T manual](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)).

In practice, then, the sample panel and controller go in the main plant room or a dosing area beside it, near the pump discharge main and the dosing pumps, and never in the chemical store _(the "never in the store" is stated; the "beside the pumps" is inferred from the short-line and close-to-pipework rules)_.

**Recommendation for the game:**

- **The sample panel and analysers belong in `dev_dosing`**, where the "Controller and sample cells" exhibit already is. The controller must sit with the dosing pumps it drives, and the flow cell must sit with the controller (short sensor cables, the calibration routine beside the test shelf). `dev_dosing` is separate from `dev_chemicals`, which matches PWTAG's segregation rule.
- **The sample take-off belongs in `dev_pumps`**, on the discharge header after the duty and standby pump sets and before the filters, upstream of any coagulant injection. It is a small fitting: a tapping with ball valve, strainer and pressure gauge, and optionally a small sample booster pump for the low-speed case TN49 describes.
- **The sample return belongs in `dev_balance`**, as a small pipe discharging into the balance tank, visible above the water so the player can see whether the sample is flowing _(inferred; a common and simple choice)_. A DPD analyser, if one is added, drains to a tundish instead.
- **Repeated in `dev_control`:** in 2000 this would be 4–20 mA signals and volt-free contacts, not a second copy of the controller _(inferred)_: free chlorine and pH on the existing chart recorders and on panel meters or LED readouts, and annunciator windows for SAMPLE FLOW LOW, CHLORINE HIGH, CHLORINE LOW, pH HIGH, pH LOW, DOSING INHIBITED and DOSING TANK LOW. The existing "Pool water controller display" in the control room could be reconsidered for the same reason.

## 5. Failure modes worth modelling

- **Sample flow lost** (strainer blocked, ball valve left shut, air lock after a pump change-over, hose kinked). The flow monitor trips after its delay and dosing stops ([W&T manual, fault table](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)). In the cell the water stands still, and the readings drift away from the pool's: chlorine in a stagnant cell is consumed, and an amperometric cell reads low without flow _(inferred)_. Without the interlock the controller would see "low chlorine" and dose without limit, which is why the input must never be bridged.
- **Low sample flow from slowed pumps.** TN49's case: readings inaccurate or the controller locks out ([TN49](https://www.pwtag.org/pool-circulation-pumps-variable-speed-drives/)).
- **Flow switch fooled.** In an investigated hotel incident, the flow switch's sensing lines did not reliably detect no-flow in the main, so chemical pumps kept running into a stopped pipe; the chemicals reacted to form chlorine, which was pushed into the pool when circulation restarted ([Processing magazine, The swimming pool case](https://www.processingmagazine.com/process-control-automation/instrumentation/flow-measurement/article/55346918/part-l-the-swimming-pool-case)). A good scenario for a fault that the panel cannot see.
- **Probe fouling and drift.** Amperometric cells drift and need grit or bead cleaning; membrane and potentiostatic probes need electrolyte topping up; probes last about two years ([TN39](https://www.pwtag.org/residual-control-september-2018/)). Shows up as a growing gap between the controller and the daily DPD test, the trigger for recalibration at 0.2 pH or 15% chlorine ([CoP §9.2](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)).
- **Reagent exhausted** (DPD analyser only): after about 30 days the reading falls to zero or freezes ([Hach CL17](https://cdn.hach.com/7FYZVWYB/at/m782tqz7nhh867zcqj7gsn3/LIT1626.pdf)); the controller then calls for full dosing unless a reagent-low alarm inhibits it _(inferred)_.
- **Take-off downstream of a dosing point.** The probes read dosed water, so chlorine reads high and the pool runs low (or acid near the tapping makes pH read low) _(inferred)_. Coagulant dosed upstream of the tapping also "could influence the measurements" ([W&T manual §5.4.6](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)).
- **Cyanuric acid** (stabilised chlorine) can make controllers inaccurate ([CoP §9.6](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191649&p=0)); unlikely in this pool, but a possible chemical delivery error.
- **Transport delay.** A long or wide sample line adds dead time and the control loop hunts ([W&T manual](https://www.trility.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Depolox-Pool-Compact-OM-Manual.pdf)).

## 6. Mapping onto the plant model (suggestions only)

Following `doc/plant-model.md` (blocks with ports, addresses like `circulation/P-101/motor:running`), a sampling system could be:

| Block (suggested address)                    | Kind                                                  | Ports in                                                               | Ports out                                                                                                                                                                      | State                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `circulation/SP-1`                           | sample tapping (ball valve, strainer, pressure gauge) | water from the pump discharge header                                   | sample water (l/h, pressure, and the water's chlorine, pH, temperature)                                                                                                        | valve open; strainer clog 0–1                                             |
| `sampling/SBP-1` (optional)                  | sample booster pump                                   | sample water; run signal                                               | sample water at raised pressure                                                                                                                                                | running, fault                                                            |
| `sampling/line`                              | sample line                                           | sample water                                                           | sample water, delayed                                                                                                                                                          | dead time from length and flow; air-locked flag                           |
| `sampling/FC-1`                              | flow cell with flow control valve and flow switch     | sample water                                                           | sample water to return; `flowOk` signal; raw probe signals                                                                                                                     | cell water (lags the inflow); grit present                                |
| `sampling/FC-1/cl`, `/ph`, `/redox`, `/temp` | probes                                                | cell water                                                             | reading (mg/l, pH, mV, °C)                                                                                                                                                     | offset and slope drift, fouling, age                                      |
| `dosing/controller`                          | controller                                            | readings; `flowOk`; circulation running; tank low; calibration entries | `chlorineDemand` (%), `acidDemand` or `co2Demand` (%); alarms (`sampleFlowLow`, `clHigh`, `clLow`, `phHigh`, `phLow`, `dosingInhibited`); 4–20 mA repeats for the control room | set points, proportional bands, flow-alarm delay timer, dosing-time timer |
| `balance/BT-1` (existing)                    | balance tank                                          | gains a sample-return inlet                                            |                                                                                                                                                                                |                                                                           |

The existing dosing pumps take `chlorineDemand` and `acidDemand` as inputs. The sample's water quality is just the pool's, carried round the circuit with a delay; until the model tracks chlorine and pH as properties of the water, the flow cell can read them from a pool-chemistry block _(design suggestion)_. The calibration action on the test shelf writes the probe's offset and slope, with the daily DPD test as the reference, so the player's routine of "test, compare, recalibrate, log" is the same one PWTAG asks of pool staff.
