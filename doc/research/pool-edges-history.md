## Before circulation: fill and empty

- Before the Ministry of Health's 1925 "Report on the Purification of Water of Swimming Baths", most British baths were filled and emptied: the water was changed at the start of the week and grew dirtier, with "first class" and "second class" days [S1].
- Some baths filtered continuously before 1920: Liverpool's first plant was installed in 1909 [S1]. Continuous filtration and chlorination became widespread in the late 1920s and early 1930s; Liverpool installed 17 plants from 1931 to 1936, with a turnover of about four hours [S1]. West Bromwich added a purification plant with duplex filters in 1934 [S2].
- Breakpoint chlorination came just after the Second World War [S1].

## Freeboard pool with a scum channel (trough)

- **Date:** in British public baths by the early 1930s at the latest: West Bromwich's new pool (1934) had a scum channel tiled to match the bath [S2]; St Annes open-air bath's scum channel doubled as an overflow, filtered water entering at the bottom pushing the surface water into it [S3]. Earlier origin (Victorian baths) **indicative**: not established by a source here.
- **How it worked:** a channel in the pool wall at water level; the water held at its lip, the surface (scum) spilling in and away; return through inlets [S3]. In UK practice the drained surface water often went to waste rather than back to the plant (**indicative**; [S3]'s channel discharged to sea).
- **Section (indicative):** water about 150–300 mm below the deck; channel about 150 mm wide, 100–150 mm deep, glazed, under an overhanging coping that serves as a handhold.
- **Problems:** level must be held at the lip; gravity gutters flood in surges and wash scum back in [S15]; hard to clean; waves rebound off the wall (**indicative**).
- US practice regarded scum gutters as the most efficient skimming and standard on pools over 1,600 sq ft [S15].

## Roll-out gutter

- **Indicative only.** No British or European source found. It appears in American practice (the gutter formed in the coping with a rolled lip). Recommend: a timeline note only, or drop.

## Skimmer (freeboard) pool

- **How it works:** a rectangular opening at the top of the wall, typically about 300 mm wide by 150 mm high (wide-angle to about 600 mm), with a hinged floating weir and a removable basket [S11]. UK freeboard pools hold the water about 150 mm below the coping; skimmers 300–500 mm wide, about 150 mm deep [S8].
- **Date:** from the mid-twentieth century in private pools (**indicative**: no dated source found for its invention).
- **Problem:** a few skimmers take less of the surface than a weir all round; the wall face shows all round [S8].
- **US state rules** (for scale, not UK): skimmers sized for 50–80 % of recirculation (Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Florida, Oregon codes, found 2026-10-28). The UK figure (PWTAG) is **not verified**.

## Deck-level edges (Agrob Buchtal's named systems)

All from [S5] unless noted; no source gives the systems' dates (1960s–70s is **indicative**).

- **Finnish (Finnland):** sloping, beach-like edge with a handhold piece; elevated water; covered channel with a coved gutter tile; tiles 150 mm and 119 mm wide in several rows; low wave rebound; for sports, competition, leisure pools [S5, S6]. The patent describes the Finnish top as a slightly inclined perimeter over which the water overflows [S7].
- **Zurich:** an elevated overflow edge that is itself the handhold piece; covered channel with a coved gutter tile; variable channel volume [S5]. One of the two conventional pool tops [S7].
- **Wiesbaden:** one large gutter stone combining handhold and channel, with a cover grating; large or small channel variants; for leisure, thermal, outdoor pools [S5]. The channel is in the stone at the pool's edge, not set back in the deck.
- (Others in [S5]: Berlin, St Moritz, Bamberg; not proposed for the hall.)
- **Return:** gravity drains to a balance tank, pumped through filters and disinfection back through inlets [S8, S9].

## Modern grated channel and balance tank

- Water laps over the coping into a grill or slot in the paving and a gutter below; "every deck level pool has a balance tank"; ten bathers displace about 750 litres [S8].
- Balance tank sizing (PWTAG code, via [S9]): bather surge on at least 50 % of the instantaneous bathing load at 80 litres a bather; maximum tank level just below the transfer channel; extraction sized for full circulation at that level; minimum level covers backwash (30 m/h per m² of filter for 5–7 minutes) without the pumps drawing air; about 2 m headroom. PWTAG Technical Note 32 covers it [S10].
- **Pros:** a long weir takes more surface water than a few skimmers; the wall is hidden [S8]. **Con:** cost and the balance tank [S8].

## Infinity (vanishing) edge

- John Lautner's Reiner House ("Silvertop"), Silver Lake, Los Angeles, is usually credited as one of the first; dates vary: 1956 [S14], 1957 (photo caption), construction to 1963 [S13], "early 1960s" [S12]. Plaque: "Silvertop, Los Angeles, designed in the late 1950s".
- Lautner drew a vanishing edge for "Diamonds Are Forever" (1971) [S12]. Seen as exotic until popular from the late 1980s [S14].
