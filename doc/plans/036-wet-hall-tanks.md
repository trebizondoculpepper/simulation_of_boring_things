Coordinates are metres in the wet hall's existing local frame: +x east, +z south; the hall's interior is approximately 19.6 × 19.6 m. ## Decision and scope

Give the **wet hall two principal tanks**: a protected clean make-up break tank in a north-side service bay, and a dirty backwash holding tank set below the south-side floor. “Clean” distinguishes the make-up stream from backwash; after the mains air gap this is pool-process water, not a drinking-water outlet. The Lounge pool's separate balance tank remains a separate pool-system project adjoining its own plant room; the spa and footbath retain their packaged vessels. No pool return or backwash pipe may connect to the mains inlet upstream of the air gap.

Use the existing 13.2 m³ operating make-up storage (`WET_HALL_PLANT.breakTank`) as the clean-tank target. Draw it as a roughly 3 × 3 × 2 m sectional, covered and insulated GRP vessel; 13.2 m³ corresponds to about 1.47 m depth, with remaining geometric volume for its inlet, overflow and freeboard. **The backwash pit holds 105 m³ to its top water level** (the owner, 2026-10-26: two of the Olympic's filters and one of the lounge's must be washable at the same time, needing 70 m³; then half as deep again, for water slides and a children's pool to come). Measured in the world, that wash sends about 49 m³ to drain in some eight minutes (two Olympic backwashes 21.2 m³ at 362 m³/h, a lounge backwash 1.7 m³, two Olympic rinses 26.5 m³ at 369 m³/h), and the 10 m³/h discharge takes away little of it meanwhile, so the pit must hold nearly all of it. The plant sizing gives the same figure (two of the biggest filters' and one of the next pool's backwash at 50 m/h for 5 minutes, a tenth more, to the next 5 m³: 70 m³, then half again: 105 m³). The pit is 7 × 5 m inside and 3.75 m deep to its covering slab; 105 m³ stands at 3.0 m, leaving 0.75 m freeboard, and the two-and-one wash fills it about halfway, with a 1.2 × 1.2 × 0.6 m settlement sump under its pump (`BACKWASH_PIT`). Its high level is 80 % (above what a two-and-one wash leaves in it from empty) and its high-high 93 %: there its controller shuts its inlet, inhibiting every backwash and rinse until its pump has brought it back below 75 %. The current 3 × 2 × 2 m sectional backwash view is only 12 m³ gross and cannot depict the pit.

The drawn rectangles are reserved envelopes, not exact mesh footprints. Preserve at least 1.2 m main circulation clearance and accessible working space at valves, pump withdrawal and tank inspection points. The partition, self-closing door and raised clean-bay threshold are design safeguards to stop splash, aerosols and a floor spill reaching the break tank; they are not claimed here as a statutory wall-separation requirement. Keep the dirty side's floor falling to its own drain and the clean bay's floor drain and overflow visibly separate from the pit inlet. The west ramp's second flight terminates near the central aisle, so do not route any tank wall, cable or pipe through that landing.

## Water and control paths

1. **Clean make-up:** town main → inlet isolator → visible, inspectable fluid-category-5 Type AA or AB air gap → covered GRP tank → low-level interlock → duty/standby booster → make-up meter → pool plant rooms. Place the potable/main-fed pipe wholly above the air gap and label the downstream circuit as non-potable process make-up. Preserve the tank's screened warning overflow and a separate maintenance drain; discharge both with an appropriate air break and away from the waste pit's open inlet.
2. **Dirty backwash:** pool plant backwash headers → air-break inlet with splash control on the dirty side → concrete pit → settlement sump → duty discharge pump and isolators → sampled/controlled foul-sewer discharge. The pit gets independent high and high-high level indications. High-high inhibits further automatic backwash and alarms; a pump fault must not silently create an uncontrolled spill into the room. The existing world model's `spilt` counter should be shown as an alarm/failure outcome rather than a normal operating path.
3. **No cross connection:** the two circuits have distinct pipes, ports, valve tags, drains and colours; no tee, hose or temporary bypass may bridge them. Route overhead distribution clear of the ramp and stair. Route gravity waste with a continuous fall and an air break instead of asking the voxel router to invent a drain slope.

## UK regulatory check and design safeguards

The sources checked so far do **not identify a blanket prohibition on locating these tanks in one hall**. This is an inference, not an approval: the installation must still prevent contamination, maintain suitable backflow protection and meet the undertaker's requirements. This plan assumes a site in England or Wales; Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own water-fittings regimes, so confirm the eventual fictional setting before treating a provision as locally applicable.

The clean-bay partition and raised threshold are conservative design choices, not an assertion that UK law always requires separate rooms. An actual installation would need local water undertaker review and wastewater-discharge consent or other authorisation as applicable. The game should depict those protective features without presenting its numbers as approved engineering values.

## Questions settled (the owner, 2026-10-26)

- **Floor gullies, each side's apart** (the owner, 2026-10-26: spills must drain): two in the make-up bay (by the break tank's warning overflow, and in front of the booster set) on their own branch to the foul drain, never to the pit; two on the dirty side (by the pit's pump, and in its south-west corner); none in the aisle (`WET_HALL_LAYOUT.gullies`).

- **The north wall is where the outside world's services come in and go out** (the owner, 2026-10-26): the town main in at the make-up bay's west end, low, and the boosted make-up main out to the pool plant rooms at its east end, high, each through a marked sleeve with a flanged stub for the plumber to join.

- **The site is in England.** England's rules are the regulatory basis: the Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999 and the Water Industry Act 1991, as in the source table above.
- **The make-up bay is a partitioned zone inside `wet_hall`, not a room of its own.** Its division may be a partition or a full wall; either way it is part of `wet_hall` and the same simulation circuit.
- **The backwash pit is serviced from above only.** There is no lower-level service access: inspection, instruments, sampling and pump withdrawal are from the floor and its guarded hatch, with no routine entry.
