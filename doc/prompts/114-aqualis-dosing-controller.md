# 114: The Aqualis dosing controller and Dr Aqualis

The owner, 2026-10-31: the plant has long needed a visible chemical dosing computer, and it now has its maker. Aqualis Interactive, the brand made for Hall 4's ClearDose Studio console (mission 113) and the company that sponsors that exhibit, also makes chemical dosing systems. Agreed over several rounds of questions; nothing is built yet. Read the root `CLAUDE.md` first (the plant model, the enhanced network, the period rule, local-only controls, alarms on both control rooms, displays redrawing only on change, input through `src/toolkit/controls`), then `doc/plant-model.md`, `doc/room-layout-guide.md` and `doc/design/control-interface-archetypes.md`. Mission 113 (`doc/prompts/113-hall-4-dosing-console.md`, its plans in `doc/plans/113/`) holds most of the parts this mission reuses.

## What it is

An Aqualis dosing controller of the early 2000s: a white cabinet, mostly touch-screen controlled, with an LCD monitor, in the Windows Vista era's look. It is a real working component modelled on real pool controllers of the period, bound to its pool's model like every other machine; a touch screen is allowed under the period rule because it brings its own kind of interface, and the plant around it keeps its single-purpose instruments.

- **The cabinet, as a block** (the owner, 2026-10-31): a white cabinet with a water inlet and a water outlet (the sampling loop's two ends) and a number of control ports, one for each dosing system it drives (a pump for hypochlorite and for acid, a valve for CO₂), and an alarm output to the annunciator. On its front: the touch screen and the HAND · OFF · AUTO knob.
- **Three models behind it** (the owner, 2026-10-31), each pure and tested on its own:
  - **The water sampler**: given the water, it returns the indicative values, every reading the analyser has (below). It knows nothing of set points or pumps.
  - **The water controller**: given the sampler's readings, its set points, the knob's position and the pool dynamics it has been given (the volume of water in circulation, the pool plus its balance tank, and the average filtration rate, both entered on its settings screen, not read from the model), it diagnoses the water and works out, at any instant, how much of each additive it would dose (the recommendations, and Show dose's amounts); and it drives whichever dosing systems are attached to its control ports.
  - **The display model**: binds the other two together into the Aqualis machine's user interface (its cycling screens, Dr Aqualis's advice and moods, the pump status, the touches it takes). Only this one is Aqualis's; the sampler and controller could serve another maker's cabinet.
- **What it is not, and what it replaces** (the owner, 2026-10-31): the chemical distribution stays as it is: the hypochlorite and acid stores, their transfer pumps and the wet hall's Chlorella transfer control box (`chemicals.ts`, `chemical-distribution.ts`, `chem-control.ts`), which keep each plant room's day tanks filled. The Aqualis cabinet is the next step down: one in each plant room that doses automatically, deciding how much of each chemical goes from that room's day tanks into its pool's water. Today that decision is made unseen, by the PI controller in `water-treatment.ts` (`CONTROLLER`: free chlorine 1.3 mg/l at the sample, pH 7.3, tuned on the chemistry bench), with no machine in the room to show or work it; the Aqualis cabinet becomes that machine, and its controller model takes over from (or is built from) that one. It also replaces the sampling bypass and water-quality controller `doc/plant-model.md` planned, of which `src/game/plant/sampling.ts` holds a first go that was never put in the plant: borrow from it where it helps (the tapping, the flow cell's lag, the probes' drift and fouling), do not shape the new models around it, and retire what is not reused once the new models exist.
- **Why it matters in the game** (the owner, 2026-10-31): first, it gives a body to what has so far been a disembodied part of the game model, the controller that decides how much hypochlorite and acid go in, so the player can see it and work it. Second, it is where the player goes in each of the big plant rooms to find out the water's condition. Third, it is where to look first when something goes wrong with the hypochlorite, acid or CO₂ system, before starting an investigation: its pump status shows which channel is not dosing, what it is asking for, and why it may not be getting it.
- **Sampling loop**: it draws water from its pool's circulation through a sampling loop and returns it to a re-injection point downstream; both are connections in the room's schematic, routed by the plumber like any other pipe.
- **Analysis (deliberate science fiction)**: until the chemical test kits exist as items, the controller's analyser measures every reading itself: free, combined and total chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, the balance index (LSI), TDS, sulphate and temperature. This is a stated exception to the no-peeking rule (`overlay-no-peeking`): do not "fix" it by making the analyser blind to readings real probes cannot measure. When test kits arrive, the owner will decide whether it keeps this power.
- **Dosing it controls** (the owner, 2026-10-31): whatever automatic dosing system it is connected to, of three kinds: sodium hypochlorite, hydrochloric acid and CO₂. It regulates each one attached to its control ports, and only those; a cabinet with no CO₂ system connected doses no CO₂. In the game world these three are the only chemicals dosed automatically. Everything else (sodium bicarbonate, soda ash, calcium chloride, thiosulphate, dilution with fresh water and the rest) is dosed by hand, on the recommendation the screen gives the operator. CO₂ dosing is one of the game's next features and does not exist yet: the controller is built to drive it, and drives hypochlorite and hydrochloric acid until it arrives. Acid and CO₂ both lower pH; where a pool has both, the plan proposes how they share the work. CO₂ has no pump: it comes from its cylinders or bulk tank through a regulator, and its dosing system is a valve controller, opening and closing (or modulating) a valve that lets CO₂ into its injection point (the owner, 2026-10-31). The controller's CO₂ channel drives that valve as its other channels drive their pumps.
- **HAND · OFF · AUTO**: a traditional three-position selector on the cabinet itself, a local control (it stays on the cabinet, not the control-room boards). AUTO: the controller holds free chlorine and pH at their set points by driving the dosing systems connected to it. HAND: the player runs those pumps from the touch screen. OFF: no dosing. In every mode it measures, alarms and advises.
- **Alarms**: it reports to its pool's mimic and to the annunciator with water-composition warnings (chlorine low or high, pH out, combined chlorine high, balance aggressive or scaling, and so on), shown on the old control room and the bridge as the alarms rule requires. Its warnings need a failure model before they say more than on and off (`status-words-need-failure-model`).
- **Where**: in each plant room with automatic dosing, beside its day tanks and dosing pumps: one for the Olympic pool and one for the Lounge pool ("the lazy pool" is the Lounge pool, the owner confirmed, 2026-10-31). Model and try it in a development room first, as every new piece of equipment is, and try its screens on their own before it goes into the plant rooms. The Olympic plant is partly automated and the Lounge mostly manual (`varied-automation`): expect the Olympic's controller to live in AUTO and the Lounge's to be run more by hand.

## Its screen and Dr Aqualis

The interface leans into early-2000s skeuomorphism: brushed metal and glass, glossy buttons, gauges drawn as dials, tabbed panels; the Frutiger Aero widget kit in `src/ui2d/aero/` is the starting point, extended as needed.

- **Its main job is AUTO** (the owner, 2026-10-31): regulating the dosing systems connected to it on its own. Everything else it does is in support of that, or of the player.
- **The screen cycles between three** (the owner, 2026-10-31): left alone, the main screen rotates between its three normal screens.
  - **Pump status**: its three automatic control channels (hypochlorite and hydrochloric acid, each a pump; CO₂, a valve), and for each what is being dosed now or what is being requested. A channel with nothing connected says so; one whose pump or valve is switched off, or set to its own HAND (or manual), or tripped or otherwise not taking the controller's demand, is shown OFFLINE with the reason as far as the controller can tell, while the controller goes on asking for what it would dose (the owner, 2026-10-31).
  - **Water status**: a readout of the water quality it is sampling, every reading with its band against its target.
  - **Dr Aqualis**: the Microsoft Agent-like helper, recommending what should be done to the water to improve its quality.

  A touch stops the cycle on the screen touched, so the player can read it, work it or ask for Show dose; the cycle resumes after a while untouched. Settings (below) is reached from any of them.

- **Settings** (the owner, 2026-10-31): a settings screen of two tabs, reached from the touch screen, never part of the cycle.
  - **Pool dynamics**: the operator enters the total volume of water in circulation and the average filtration rate, from which the controller works out how quickly and how much to dose (the turnover sets how long a dose takes to reach the pool and the probes). The values are what was entered, right or wrong: a controller set up with the wrong volume over- or under-doses, and its Show dose amounts are wrong with it. Each installation comes set up correctly for its pool.
  - **Targets**: the target for every reading the analyser has, editable, its set point and band. Defaults after PWTAG: free chlorine 1.2 mg/l, held between 1.0 and 1.5; pH 7.3, held between 7.2 and 7.4; the other readings' bands from the water chemistry model's own. AUTO doses in proportion to how far a reading is from its set point, stopping inside its band and capped at each pump's rated output. The diagnosis and recommendations judge the water against these targets too.
- **The game loop** (the owner, 2026-10-31): the player comes to this screen for clues to what will put the water right. Dr Aqualis's diagnosis is the clue, Show dose the answer for anyone stuck; the fixing is the player's (the automatically dosed chemicals by AUTO or HAND, the rest by hand).

- **Diagnosis and direction, not prescriptions** (the owner chose this): the advice says what is wrong and which chemical puts it right ("Alkalinity low (62 mg/l): the pH will be unstable. Raise the alkalinity with sodium bicarbonate."), not how much. A **Show dose** button reveals the exact amount for anyone stuck, worked out through the real chemistry model at the pool's real volume (the Hall 4 dose table and solver, `src/game/plant/museum-4-doses.ts`, `museum-4-solver.ts`, are the place to start; the amounts belong in that chemical's own unit). Advice for hand-dosed chemicals points at where they go: the balance tank, or the pool itself.
- **Dr Aqualis**, an animated helper in the spirit of Microsoft Agent, an original character and not a copy of any Microsoft one: an anthropomorphic water drop with eyes and a face (the owner, 2026-10-31): the Aqualis water-drop logo come to life, a glossy, blobby, animated blue droplet in a white lab coat with a head mirror and a stethoscope. He gives the advice in written speech bubbles only: no narration and no synthesised speech (the owner, 2026-10-31). Animations, at no more than 10 Hz and redrawn only while they play: idle (a gentle bob), concerned (a frown, tapping his clipboard), alarmed (wide eyes, a little jump), pleased (a thumbs up when the water is good), pointing (at the reading his advice is about).
- **Readings**: what the three screens show between them: every reading the analyser has, with its band and target, each channel's state and demand, and the selector's position.

## The hand-dosing game to come (not this mission)

Later, a separate mission: the player picks up containers of chemicals into an inventory, carries them, and adds them to a balance tank or straight into a pool. The controller's advice is written for that game ("add sodium bicarbonate to the balance tank"); until it exists, hand doses can be given the way Hall 4 gives them, or by a stand-in the implementer proposes to the owner first.
