## The real pumps compared

What a UK pool would choose from for 10 to 30 l/h of 14 % hypochlorite against 1.5 to 3 bar at the injection point. Hypochlorite's needs: it decomposes, giving off oxygen, which collects in a diaphragm pump's head and gas-locks it (PWTAG wants it dosed "under flooded suction with some means of removing any oxygen gas", CoP §12.12); and it wants PVDF or PVC heads, PTFE diaphragms and seals, ceramic or PTFE balls, FKM or EPDM O-rings.

## The recommendation

**For a real UK pool** the safest choice for 14 % hypochlorite at 12.5 l/h is a peristaltic Qdos 30 (it cannot gas-lock) or a stepper pump with automatic deaeration (DDA 17-7 FCM, which also says what is wrong in plain words).

**For the game** both already exist: Grundflow's PERI 30 (30 l/h, after the Qdos; dev44) and DDS 7.5-16 (after the DDA; the acid boards). Putting either in would fix the Olympic, but adds no new kind of interaction. So the design is the plant's third kind of metering pump, **a motor-driven diaphragm pump after the ProMinent Sigma/ 1**:

- It is the right size: 21 l/h at full stroke, commissioned with its dial at 60 % so its most is 12.6 l/h, the sizing, which also caps what a runaway controller could put in.
- It fits a plant of about 2000 (Sigma's predecessors were in pool plant rooms then), and it brings controls the plant does not yet have: a mechanical stroke length dial, a seven-segment LED fascia, a bleed valve worked by hand, a flow monitor's blinking lamp, a rupture indicator's window.
- Its weakness is the interesting one: gas. A diaphragm pump on hypochlorite gas-locks, and a gas-locked pump strokes, sounds and shows its rate while delivering nothing; the control room sees chlorine sag and the dosing-time alarm, and the player must go to the plant room, see the flow monitor's lamp no longer blinking (or `Er F`), open the bleed valve, and reset at the pump. A degassing head (a bleed orifice to the return at every stroke, about 7 % of its liquid) makes it rare.

**One pump, not a duty pair** (the owner, 2026-10-28): the DMH replaces the Olympic's pump one for one. The owner's reasoning: it is simpler to replace what we have, and dosing does not need a standby, even in the Olympic, because the fallback is to dose the balance tank by hand (TODO, "Hand dosing at every balance tank"), so two pumps are not critical. The Olympic's set is drawn today as a pair of DMX 1602s (PL2-DP1, `createHypochloriteBoard`); the replacement is still a single unit. On a fault the pump's error relay goes to the dosing controller and the annunciator only; there is no changeover and no weekly swap, and the operator's fallback is hand dosing at the balance tank until the pump is put right.

## The design

**The maker.** Grundflow, the game's pump maker (the owner, 2026-10-28, on seeing the first pictures: Grundflow's, not a new brand), in its livery, badge and rating plate; named into its range as its others are (DMX 1602, DDS 7.5-16, PERI 30): DMH 21-10, after Grundfos's own motor-driven DMH. A bought-in pump carries its own maker's badge and rating plate, not Penfold's. (A first draft gave it a maker of its own, Hubwerk Dosiertechnik; dropped at the owner's word.)

**The product.** Grundflow DMH 21-10 PVT: 21 l/h at 10 bar, 90 strokes a minute of 3.9 ml (a 4 mm stroke), PVDF head, PTFE diaphragm and seals, ceramic balls, 230 V 110 W IP65, about 340 × 170 × 300 mm. Its motor at the right (ribbed, fan cowl, terminal box, cable gland), its worm gearbox in the middle with the stroke dial on top, its head at the left; its control module on the motor.

**Its state** (`src/toolkit/components/plant/logic/motor-dosing-pump.ts`, `MotorPumpState`): `on` (STOP/START), `mode` (MAN, its own rate; EXT, 4–20 mA), `manual` (its stroke rate, 5 % a press), `strokeLength` (the dial, 5 % a detent), `bleedOpen`, `shows` (l/h, SPM, %, mA), `head` (standard or degassing), `gasMl` (gas in its head), `short` (strokes in a row its flow monitor found short), `error` (flow, level, diaphragm, or none), `ruptured`, and totals delivered and returned. Derived: running, strokes a minute (stroke rate × 90), its set rate (stroke rate × stroke length × 21 l/h), gas-locked or not, leak (a ruptured diaphragm), fault (its error).

**How it works, stroke by stroke** (`stepMotorPump`, `oneStroke`): each stroke must compress the head's gas from about 1 bar absolute to the discharge pressure before its outlet valve lifts (`gas × (1 − 1/P)` of the stroke); what is left is delivered, carrying gas out in proportion to the head's. When the gas takes the whole stroke the pump is gas-locked. Its bleed valve open sends the stroke to the return at no pressure, gas first: priming. A degassing head's orifice vents 0.35 ml of gas a stroke for 7 % of its liquid. Against a closed discharge its integral relief valve lifts at 11 bar and the stroke returns. The flow monitor stops it after 20 short strokes in a row (`Er F`, latched until STOP/START at the pump: a local reset). EMPTY on its level input stops it (`Er L`, clearing when filled); LOW lights WARN. A ruptured diaphragm turns its indicator red and stops it (`Er d`) until the diaphragm is changed. Its display shows the rate it is set to, never what it delivers: it does not know it is gas-locked.

**How the controller drives it.** In EXT, the controller's Yout (0 to 1) as 4–20 mA scales its stroke rate (0 to 90 a minute; below 4.1 mA it pauses), and the controller's release contact lets it run; its error relay tells the controller (and the annunciator) it has stopped. In the world the dosing controller's Yout would become `4 + 16 × Yout` mA; at the dial's 60 % the most is 12.6 l/h, and the Olympic's average 2.8 l/h is about 22 % (about 20 strokes a minute).

**Its interface**, deliberately unlike the DoseCell (16 × 2 LCD) and Grundflow's pumps (blue dot-matrix LCD, colour TFT): a 1990s membrane fascia on a grey control module, Grundflow blue round a black panel: a four-digit red seven-segment LED readout (`3.15`, `StoP`, `PAUS`, `Er F`); L/H, SPM, % and mA lamps for what it shows; MAN and EXT; RUN (lit while it runs, dipping at each stroke), WARN and ERR; keys STOP/START, P, i, ▲, ▼; its error codes printed above the readout. Mechanical controls: the stroke dial (a ribbed black knob over a printed 0–100 % scale, self-locking) and the red quarter-turn BLEED knob on the head (anticlockwise opens). Indicators on the head: the flow monitor's green lamp round the discharge valve, blinking for each stroke delivered; the rupture indicator's green window, red once burst.

**Its sound** (`dosing-pump-sounds.ts`): held while it runs, `MOTOR_DIAPHRAGM_PUMP`, its four-pole motor's 100 Hz hum and 24 Hz shaft beat under a faint 480 Hz gear whine; each stroke heard as it comes (`MOTOR_PUMP_STROKE`): a firm knock with a thump when it delivers, a hollow light tick when gas-locked (the sound of a pump that has lost its prime), a wet gurgle while bleeding. At about 20 strokes a minute it is a slow, heavy beat, quite unlike the solenoid's tick.

**Its fittings and valves for the schematic**, each a fitting of the Olympic's chemical schematic (`olympic-chemical-schematic.ts`) with its function, normal state and tag, before anything is drawn:

- Suction, from the hypochlorite day tank (flooded): the day tank's outlet isolation (exists, PL2.hypochlorite-day.out.iso); a calibration column on a three-way valve (normal: to the tank); the pump's suction isolation (normal open).
- The pump's integral relief and bleed valve (normal shut), its return line back to the day tank's top (a new link, `hypochlorite return`), entering above the liquid.
- Discharge: the pump's discharge isolation (normal open); its pulsation damper (a gas-cushioned PVDF vessel, 0.5 l, needed for a diaphragm pump's pulses; normal in service) with a pressure gauge on a diaphragm seal; then its back-pressure valve (1.5 bar, so the valves seat and nothing siphons) and the dose line to the injection quill, whose injection valve is its non-return (exists, PL2.hypo.out.nrv).
- **Tags**, hierarchical and derived (`model-valves.ts`): the set PL2-DP1; its one pump PL2-DP1-P1; the pump's valves PL2-DP1-P1-IV1 (suction), -IV2 (discharge), -RV1 (relief and bleed), -PD1 (pulsation damper), -PI1 (gauge); PL2-DP1-BPV1 (back pressure), PL2-DP1-CC1 and its three-way valve PL2-DP1-V3W1. `ModelValve.kind` takes only isolating, power-isolator and non-return today: relief, back-pressure, three-way and the damper are new kinds for it (or equipment, not valves: an open question below).

## Open questions for the owner (ask one at a time)

Answered by the owner, 2026-10-28: the maker is Grundflow, not a new brand; the fascia's lettering smaller; one pump, not a duty pair; and the pumps a family on the ideal pump (below).

1. Install the DMH, or put in the existing PERI 30 (peristaltic, which cannot gas-lock) or DDS (after the DDA) instead? The DMH is designed for the gas problem's gameplay; the others are what a cautious engineer would fit.
2. Standard heads or degassing heads? Degassing makes gas-locking rare (only a big slug or a closed bleed on a long standstill); standard makes it a regular chore.
3. Should the stroke dial be adjustable only while the pump runs, as the real manual says?
4. Are relief valves, back-pressure valves, dampers and calibration columns valves in the model (new `ModelValve` kinds) or equipment of the pump set?
5. Should the DMH's display be calibrated (a calibration routine with the column, so it shows what it truly delivers), or stay showing its nominal rate?
