## What the hall teaches, and what it builds on

**Assumes everything before** (Halls 1 to 4 as planned; whatever Halls 5 to 12 add is folded in here when their themes are set): the pump, flow and pressure, the boiler and exchanger, the thermostat and the interlock, losses (Hall 1); the balance tank, the filter, its fouling and backwash, the dead-head trip (Hall 2); the sample, the controller, the metering pumps and their faults (Hall 3); the water's chemistry and its treatments (Hall 4).

**Adds:**

- **The plant as one system:** every part in its place in a single loop, each affecting the others (a dirty filter cuts the flow, which starves the exchanger, which lets the pool cool; a drifted probe overdoses).
- **Reading the plant:** the mimic as the map, the annunciator as the first warning, the first-out window as the first cause; accept, then find, then fix, then reset.
- **Cause and effect:** a symptom on one instrument, its cause somewhere else; diagnose from the plant's own displays, as a real operator must (no peeking).
- **Local and central:** the desk shows everything and works what its mimic carries (pump START and STOP, valve OPEN and SHUT where they are motorised), but resets, mode selectors and hand valves are at the machines.

**Ends the route:** the closing wall (below) answers Hall 0's welcome, and the way out is straight on, into Hall 0.

## The water's readouts and alarms (the owner, 2026-10-28)

"Let's have some read-outs, for example a display showing the chemical values of the water, and an annunciator showing some of the failure conditions, e.g. high pH, low pH."

- **A water-quality display** on the control desk: free chlorine, pH, and the other values the model measures by instrument (temperature, flow), each read from its probe or transmitter, never from the model's true water (the no-peeking rule). A drifted probe therefore shows its drifted value.
- **The annunciator carries the water's alarms** beside the plant's: CHLORINE HIGH, CHLORINE LOW, pH HIGH, pH LOW, SAMPLE FLOW LOW, POOL TEMP HIGH, POOL TEMP LOW, each lit by its instrument's reading crossing its limit (the limits from 107c's PWTAG figures: chlorine below 0.5 or above 2.0 mg/l, pH below 7.2 or above 7.6, indicative bands to be confirmed against 107c's research).
- **The drifted probe (fault 10), so answered:** no window is lit from the true water. The probe reads low, so the display shows chlorine looking fine or low, the controller doses more, and if the probe's reading falls far enough CHLORINE LOW may even light, the opposite of the truth. The visitor finds the fault only by testing with TK1 and seeing it disagree with the display, then calibrating DC1. The fault table's "CHLORINE HIGH only on TK1" means only that the kit shows it, not a window.

### Plaque facts, with sources

- "Free chlorine 1.0 mg/l is only 50 % effective at pH 7.5, and each 0.5 rise in pH roughly doubles the chlorine you need." (PWTAG CoP §6, via `water-chemistry-models.md`.)
- "Automatic control does not measure combined chlorine: staff test by hand, several times a day." (PWTAG CoP §9.2.)
- "The flow switch on the sample cell stops the dosing. Without it, a dry cell would read 'no chlorine' and the controller would dose without limit." (`water-sampling.md`, W&T DEPOLOX manual.)
- "Variable-speed pumps can starve the sample cell; a small sample pump fixes it." (PWTAG TN49.)
- "Water should be clear enough to see the pool floor; PWTAG's limit is 0.5 NTU." (PWTAG CoP §5.)
- "A pool's managers must have written operating procedures and alarm responses." (HSG179, normal operating procedures and emergency action plan, _indicative wording_.)

### Research conflicts

- **Fault 10, CHLORINE HIGH "only on TK1".** An annunciator window driven by DC1's own reading cannot light, since DC1 reads low. The brief's table and Try-it 3 ("no window at all until the free chlorine passes 2.5") disagree: the second implies a window lit from the true value, which would be peeking. Recommend: no window ever for this fault; found only by TK1 (keeping the overlay rule).
- **Fault 5, "B1 idle".** A boiler with its own flow thermostat will still fire to temperature and then cycle, not sit idle, when the secondary valve is shut; on the mimic it shows short-cycling, TI3 at setpoint. Minor; the test should expect cycling or idle per Hall 1's model.
- **Fault 3, then "the interlocks".** HEATING NO FLOW and SAMPLE FLOW FAIL follow a pump trip, so their windows light after PUMP TRIPPED; first-out must be PUMP TRIPPED, as the brief has it. No conflict, but the test should pin the order.
