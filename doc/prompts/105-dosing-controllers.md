## The request

The owner, 2026-10-28: two dosing controllers, one for each pool group, to fit the site's plan that the Olympic is the more automated and the Lounge the more manual. The Olympic (`pool_02`) gets a modern multi-channel touch-screen controller, after the ProMinent DULCOMARIN 3 or the W&T DEPOLOX Pool; the Lounge (`pool_01`) a period pair of single-channel controllers after the ProMinent DULCOMETER D1C (chlorine and pH, about 2000), the hands-on one: the player checks it against the test kit, nudges setpoints and drops a pump into MANUAL. Mission 101 put a hidden controller into the world (`CONTROLLER` and `stepControl` in `src/game/plant/water-treatment.ts`); these replace it.

The owner's further rulings, 2026-10-28:

1. **Chlorella's.** Both controllers are Chlorella Chemicals' products, like the chemical transfer terminals and control box in the wet hall: green livery, Chlorella's mark, tag and rating plates. Like the transfer system, they are specialist supplier equipment, and so **an exception to CLAUDE.md's rule that control panels are Penfold's**. Bought-in parts inside them (the probes) keep their own makers.
2. **A sample line of water, in and out.** Each controller has its sample cell (flow cell), fed by a small sample line from the circulation (its "in") and returning to the balance tank (its "out"); on the cell a sample isolating valve, a flow regulator, the sample flow switch, the probe holders (free chlorine, pH, redox, temperature) and a manual sample tap for the DPD test.
3. **Signals to the pumps.** Signal cables run from the controller to each dosing pump (4–20 mA, or pulse, plus the release contact, and the pump's error relay back), drawn as cables in conduit or trunking.
4. **Signals to the control room.** Signal cables run along the wall, in trunking or conduit, carrying the chlorine and pH alarm relays, sample flow fail, the dosing time limit and controller fault to the control room. By the owner's standing rule, any warning or error state also updates the bridge.

## The design

**One controller model, two faces.** `src/toolkit/components/plant/logic/dosing-controller.ts` (pure, tested): generalised from the world's `CONTROLLER`, so that with `WORLD_SETTINGS`, ideal probes and nothing wrong its outputs are `stepControl`'s step by step (a test holds this, through setpoint, the lockout and circulation stops). It takes its sample cell's signals (`CellSignals`: free and total chlorine in µA, pH in mV, redox in mV, the Pt1000's °C, the flow switch) and the circulation, each pump's error relay and each day tank's empty switch; it gives each channel's output Yout (0 to 1, as `4 + 16 × Yout` mA) and release, its relays, its alarm list, its trend, and its readings through its calibration. Its state: setpoints and limits (`ControllerSettings`), each channel started or stopped, AUTO or MANUAL and its manual output, chlorine's integral, the calibration of each probe and its last record, a calibration under way, the sample-flow, feed-delay, dosing-time and limit timers, the alarm list (raised, cleared, acknowledged) and a twelve-hour trend at a point a minute.

- **Control**: PI on free chlorine (set 1.3 mg/l at the sample, band 0.5, Tn 6 h), P on pH (set 7.3, band 0.3, acid above it), the world's.
- **Interlocks**: sample flow lost for its delay (120 s) stops all dosing; circulation stopped; chlorine locked out with pH beyond setpoint ± 0.4; the maximum dosing time (2 h at 100 % in AUTO latches that output off until acknowledged); a feed delay (60 s) after a start, a calibration or a restored flow; calibration holds the outputs at 0, as the D1C's.
- **Alarms**: chlorine low (0.8) and high (2.5), pH low (7.0) and high (7.6), each after a 300 s checkout time; sample flow; circulation; dosing time per chemical; pump fault and tank empty per chemical; the pH lockout as a note. Relays: CHLORINE ALARM, pH ALARM, SAMPLE FLOW FAIL, DOSING TIME LIMIT, CONTROLLER FAULT (a pump's error relay, a tank empty, the circulation, a channel stopped), and a common alarm relay that an acknowledgement drops out.
- **Calibration**: chlorine zero (sample shut, the probe consumes the standing water's chlorine; refused beyond ±5 µA) and slope against DPD 1 (or DPD 3 for total; refused below 0.04 mg/l or outside 25–300 % of nominal); pH two-point on recognised buffers (4, 7, 10; refused closer than 2, a zero beyond ±60 mV or a slope outside 46–70 mV/pH) or one point against phenol red (its zero only).

**The sample cell** (`sample-cell.ts`, pure): the line's isolating valve and flow regulator (0 to 60 l/h), a rotameter whose reed switch makes at 20 l/h, the cell's water following the sample through the line's 30 s and its own volume's lag; standing, the probes consume its free chlorine (about 4 min). Ideal probes by the owner's ruling, with a drifted set for the bench (chlorine 70 % sensitive, pH 18 mV off and 94 % slope, redox 25 mV low). Its tap draws a sample for the photometer (DPD 1, DPD 3, phenol red).

**The Olympic's face: Chlorella CX-7** (`cx7-hmi.ts`, pure; `views/cx7-controller.ts`): a 430 × 290 × 150 mm grey body, a Chlorella-green front, a 7" touch screen (800 × 480) drawn as a 2010s plant panel: light grey ground, white tiles, dark figures, a green title bar with the clock and a flashing ALARM count; HOME (free and combined chlorine, pH, redox, temperature, each with setpoint, limits and an OK/LOW/HIGH chip, and a dosing tile: each output's bar, AUTO or MANUAL, and why it is held), CHLORINE and pH (setpoint, Xp, Tn or lockout, limits by − and +; output %, mA, AUTO | MANUAL, manual output, START/STOP DOSING, time at 100 %), TRENDS (1, 6 or 12 h of free and combined chlorine and pH against their setpoints), ALARMS (time, text, state, cleared; ACKNOWLEDGE), CALIBRATE (four procedures with their last record; a guided wizard with the live signal and reading, the entry by − and +, ACCEPT, SKIP ZERO, CANCEL, and a plain-text result or refusal) and STATUS (each interlock, the dosing-time limit adjustable, each output and its mA and release, the six relays' lamps, STOP ALL). It uses the touch-surface kit (`createTouchSurface`) with its own painter, not `ui2d`'s LCARS components; touches sound the game's touch bleeps. It is a modern item with its own kind of interface, and does not take over the plant's single-purpose instruments.

**The Lounge's face: a pair of Chlorella DC-1s** (`dc1-face.ts`, pure; `views/dc1-controller.ts`): two single-channel units on a backplate, CHLORINE (yellow band) and pH (red band), pale green clamshells with darker printed fascias as the OT-216's; a four-digit red LED readout (`createSevenSegment`, with `i`, `n`, `u`, `G` and `Y` added for `Hi`, `HAnd`, `Auto`, `Good`, `bUSY`); LEDs MEAS, OUT %, SET, AUTO, MAN, STOP, DOSE, LIMIT, ALARM; six grey membrane keys (`membrane-key.ts`) with their legends above: ◄► (CHANGE), ▲, ▼, ↩ (BACK), ↵ (ENTER) and START/STOP. CHANGE steps the reading, the output (`H 25` in MANUAL), `SEt`, `Lo`, `Hi`, the mode (`Auto`, `HAnd`) and `CAL`; ENTER opens a value (it flashes), ▲▼ change it, ENTER saves, BACK leaves it. On the reading, alarm codes alternate with the value (`E FL`, `E dt`, `E Lo`, `E Hi`, `E PU`, `E tA`, `E Ci`, `PAUS`, `StoP`); ENTER acknowledges. Chlorine calibration `CAL0` then `dPd` by ▲▼; pH `bUF1`, `bUF2`; `Good`, or `Err0`, `ErrS`, `Errd`, `Errb`. One model behind both units: the pH unit's limit relay is wired to the chlorine unit's pause input, which is the model's lockout.

A console handle, `window.dosingTrial`, sets the faults for pictures.

Checked in the browser: with the Olympic's suction shut the chlorine sags, Yout winds to 100 %, and after two hours at full the dosing time trips (`Maximum dosing time, hypochlorite: check pump`, CHLORINE low after it); a soda ash slug locks chlorine out (`PAUS` on the DC-1, `E Hi` on the pH unit); drifted probes overdose (DPD 1 reads 1.40 where the controller reads 0.94); the CX-7's chlorine calibration (zero with the sample shut, slope against the photometer's DPD 1) brings its reading back; the DC-1 pH unit's two-buffer calibration finds slope 94 %, zero 18 mV, and its reading back to the water's.

A finding for the touch-surface kit: a screen lies in its frame's face, and a ray can strike the frame first at the same distance, so a use on the screen is lost; fixed in the kit itself (2026-10-28): a use on a frame within a screen's opening (a hair's tolerance past its edge, a curved strip's point unbent first) is a touch on that screen at that point (`frameTouch`, `unbend` in `industrial-control/logic/touch-surface.ts`), for every touch panel, and the CX-7's own workaround removed.
