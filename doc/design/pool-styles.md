# Five styles of pool room

The pool rooms parody the internet's "poolrooms": endless, empty, tiled pools and passages that no architect would build, as seen in liminal-space art and games. Each style below takes one recognisable trope of the genre and plays it straight, then pricks it with the mundane: council-pool signage, municipal fittings, the faint sense that somebody has to maintain all this. That somebody is the player, through the Authorised Personnel Only door that every style has somewhere.

The styles share a vocabulary (small square tiles on every surface, water, no people) and differ in everything else: palette, light, scale and shape. A player should know which style they are in at a glance. The cues come from studying the genre (`doc/research/` notes the sources); none of them copies a particular game's rooms, layouts or assets.

## 1. The Municipal Infinite

The trope: the pool that goes on for ever. A council leisure pool of about 1975, extended without end: two-tone tiling (dark green or black mosaic to head height, white above), every corner rounded, kidney-shaped pools stepping down in tiers between ribbon decks, stainless handrails following every curve, single fat round columns standing in the water, round porthole lamps set in the walls and small square downlights in a tiled ceiling. The parody is that it is still run by the council: NO BOMBING · NO PETTING · NO RUNNING signs every few metres, a lost-property box, a timetable for Ladies' Only sessions that never come, a lifeguard's chair facing a wall. Lit by its own fittings, warm and slightly too dim.

## 2. The Porthole Tunnel

The trope: the passage that never ends. Barrel-vaulted tunnels lined entirely in small square tiles, curving floor into wall into ceiling, with oval lamps glowing in the walls at a steady pitch, often a channel of water down the middle or water underfoot, ending in a round-headed archway with light beyond; sometimes narrowing until the walker must squeeze, sometimes a long tiled stair climbing in a tunnel of its own. A flooded variant is an ordinary hotel corridor, carpet under the water, numbered doors nearly all locked, a pool ring drifting. The parody is the signage of a transport system: WAY OUT arrows pointing both ways, a MIND THE STEP plate on a step that is not there, a tunnel number stencilled every hundred metres. It joins the other styles.

## 3. The Reservoir

The trope: the vast, dark, flooded hall. Black water stretching away under a forest of huge round columns, some tapering, the far walls lost in the dark; light only from slits high up and from a few lamps on the columns; a timber boardwalk crossing it on posts. The parody: it is a municipal reservoir pretending to be a pool. There are lane ropes across it, depth markings on its columns (1.0 m, 2.0 m, 4.0 m: deep, but no deeper than its plant could heat), and a single lifeguard's high chair, far too tall, its ladder disappearing upward. Quiet, cold, echoing.

## 4. The White Room

The trope: the bright, white, misty dreamcore space. Overexposed: white tile, white light, white haze, curved walls with no visible corners, so that distance is hard to judge; white tiled stairs rising through white shafts with stainless rails, landing above landing. A single object in each room: a diving board over a pool whose bottom cannot be seen, a drinking fountain, one plastic chair. The parody: the haze is plainly steam from an over-warm pool (the plant room's fault), and the single object is labelled like an exhibit in a gallery ("Diving Board, 1983. Concrete, steel, vinyl"). Bright, calm and unnerving.

## 5. The Slide Atrium

The trope: the water park with no visitors. A tall tiled atrium, many storeys of galleries and white railings round it, kidney-shaped splash pools far below, and the one saturated colour in the pool rooms: red, yellow and blue slides and tubes weaving down through it, passing through walls, crossing each other, one looping back onto itself, some ending in mid-air. The parody: queue barriers zig-zagging to nowhere, a height-restriction sign (YOU MUST BE THIS TALL) with its arm far too high, a RIDE CLOSED sign on a chain. It is how the player moves between levels quickly, by riding.

## What the genre does that ours did not

Studying the genre showed what the first blockout lacked: curves (it was all right angles), the two-tone wall, fat round columns, tight passages between big rooms, and light from fittings (portholes, downlights, light from beyond an archway) rather than from the sun. Scale swings hard, from tunnels barely wider than the walker to halls whose far wall is lost; light swings as hard, from pitch dark to white haze. A few white plastic chairs, abandoned anywhere, do more than any amount of furniture.

## How they connect

The five styles are zones of the pool rooms (`poolrooms-layout.md`), joined by the Porthole Tunnel and by slides, on several levels: the White Room high up, the Municipal Infinite at ground, the Reservoir below, the Slide Atrium spanning them. Each has its own staff door to the plant rooms and a water treatment display board by each pool, and each shows the plant's outputs (`two-realms.md`) in its own way: the Municipal Infinite's water clouding, the White Room's haze thickening, the Reservoir's level dropping.
