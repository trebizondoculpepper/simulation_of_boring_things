# Filters, fault lights, and a plant you can read

The Pool is becoming easier to understand and more interesting to operate. Since the previous devlog, the machinery has gained clearer descriptions, filters that need cleaning, and lighting controls with consequences. Today’s additions include a working tank-hall drain and two new camera moves through the building.

## Reading the plant

Point at equipment to see its reference number, maker and model where known, equipment type, and current status. Individual controls and instruments can identify themselves too: a valve reports its position, a flowmeter reports its reading, and each boiler has its own description and operating state. The information overlay follows what the equipment can actually show.

The lounge filter panel now has a working mimic, with moving lights along its flow paths, valve controls and flow readings. It is much easier to follow where the water is going while changing the plant’s configuration.

## Filters that need looking after

The lounge, Olympic pool and spa filters now share a developing sand-filter model. Dirt increases resistance, backwashing washes it out, and rinsing settles the bed before service resumes. The spa cabinets can run their own wash programmes, sending dirty water to waste.

The cabinets also have a proper start-up: booting, self-test, then their motorised valves opening in sequence before the pump runs. Their displays, lamps and sounds follow that process, and a fault can stop it.

## Switch off the lights

The switch room’s lighting board now has individual RCBOs with working test buttons and trip indications. Damp conditions can cause leakage and a trip. When normal lighting fails, the central battery supplies dim emergency lighting while it has charge.

The balance-tank hall now follows the lighting controls too. Its ordinary lamps and glowing batten covers switch with the Olympic plant circuit.

## A more complete tank hall

The Olympic balance tank has a grated floor drain, operated by a handwheel on the gantry. It drains by gravity, including with the circulation pumps stopped. The ladder-access gate opens with Use and closes itself afterwards, and the ultrasonic level transmitter can be inspected for its live depth reading. Its cable now rises overhead towards the pool controls.

The two submerged elbows are identified as pump suction intakes. Both overflow returns are labelled as belonging to the Olympic pool; the lounge retains its separate balance tank.

## New views and smaller fixes

There are two new title shots: a switch-room flyby with normal lighting off and the flashlight on, and an underwater Olympic-pool dolly zoom with a roll, rising towards the surface as the lens tightens.

The Olympic UV unit now faces into the room, the heat-exchanger valves clear their elbows, and pump bolts are shorter and hexagonal. Equipment signs and fittings have had mounting and overlap fixes, and the HUD is less cluttered. New games also start from a checked, healthy operating state, ready to explore.

The tank-hall gate works, but climbing down its ladder is still to come.
