## Headline

- **The rule is in `CLAUDE.md`** (the owner approved it, 2026-10-28): displays redraw only when what they show changes; view state from the world is worked out once per world step; self-animating displays at no more than 10 Hz; new displays run through the benchmark and kept within its budget, or listed with a reason.
- **The budgets fail loudly.** Any component over budget and not listed in `KNOWN_OVER_BUDGET` (`src/toolkit/gallery/logic/component-bench.ts`, each with its reason) is a `console.error`, a red banner over the page and the page's title (`Component bench: FAILED, n over budget`); `window.componentBench.verdict` has the failures. The report says which textures a component uploads (`uploading 704×320 ×2`). This run: **passed**, 403 components, none over budget unexplained.
- **The biggest per-frame update costs left in the rooms were all one mistake, repeated: deriving the same view state again every frame.** `identifiedPart` walked the whole plant model (with its validation) on every call, and the foot spa's cabinet called it about a dozen times a frame; the Olympic battery's flows were worked out by each of five sand filters every frame; the lounge filter mimic's state by each of its two boards. Each is now cached on the immutable world or model root. The lounge pump panel 139 → 35 µs a frame, the foot spa cabinet 269 → 58, the jacuzzi's 111 → 25, an Olympic sand filter 198 → 14 (each timed alone, back to back).
- **The sand filters' sight glasses and POOLMON no longer upload with nothing changing.** Seven sight glasses at 13 uploads a second each (0.87 M texels a second each, about 6 M in all) were easing from a clean bed toward their real state for half a minute after every room build; they now start settled. POOLMON's cursor blink re-uploaded its 640 × 500 screen twice a second; the cursor is now a layer of its own. Both now 0 with the world still.
- **Small seven-segment displays draw at the texel density of the large ones**: at least 8 texels a millimetre, as a boiler's 26 mm digits already had, not four times over regardless of size. The spa UV's 12 mm hours counter, which changes every second, uploads 432 × 160 instead of 864 × 320: the spa and jacuzzi cabinets 690k → 172k texels a second running. Compared side by side close up, small and large look equally sharp.
- **Draw calls still dominate**, as the first report found: the top of the ranking (the Wash-O-Tron, the backwash tank, the pump panel, the booster set, the pump pair) is their render, hundreds of draw calls each. That work (merging, presets) is outside this mission.

## Budgets that fail loudly

`DEFAULT_BUDGET` is unchanged (still: at most 12 uploads and 250,000 texels a second with the world still; 300,000 texels a second running; an update at most 200 µs). What is new:

- `budgetVerdict(samples)` sorts every component over budget into **failures** and **known** (listed in `KNOWN_OVER_BUDGET` by id or id prefix, each with its reason: flagged in `TODO.md`, or left because it is a real change). A failure is a `console.error` naming the component and what it is over, a red banner at the top of the page, and the title `Component bench: FAILED, n over budget`; a pass is a green banner and `done, within budget`.
- Each over-budget line ends with the textures it uploads, by size, most first (`uploading 384×192 ×4`), which found the spa's hours counter and POOLMON's cursor.
- So that a failure is the component's and not the machine's: the update's cost is the **median of six batches** of twenty calls (one batch caught by a collection or the development server's reload had been reading 1–7 ms for the wet hall's machines, which take 3–40 µs); and a component measured with the page **starved of frames** (fewer than 20 a second in either phase, as when a game ran in another tab) is measured again up to twice, and if still starved is listed as not judged rather than failed.
- Not in CI: the numbers need a GPU and a browser. The rule in `CLAUDE.md` makes rerunning it part of adding a display.
