## Headline

- **Display work is not where the frame goes; draw calls are.** Measured component by component, a component's own render (CPU side: draw-call submission, which includes its texture uploads) costs about 15 µs a draw call on this machine, and the big machines are hundreds of draw calls each: the lounge's local control panel 336, a pair of circulation pumps 384, the Wash-O-Tron 323, the backwash tank 366. Those dominate the ranking by cost. Their updates are a tenth of that or less. This agrees with the research: the frame rate players see is the GPU and draw-call work (graphics presets, merging), not displays.
- **The chart recorders were the worst display, as the owner suspected.** The control room's circular chart uploaded a 512 × 512 texture every frame: 60 uploads and 15.7 million texels a second with nothing changing, by far the largest upload of any component in the game's rooms (the next, a sand filter's sight glass, 0.87 million). Fixed: it now inks and uploads only when a pen has moved a pixel; with the world still, none. The chart recorders panel went from 66.6 uploads and 15.9 M texels a second to 6.7 and 0.43 M (what is left is its two strip charts' paper creeping at 4 Hz, which is the picture changing).
- **The DoseCell heads were the dearest per-frame update in the rooms**: each of the eight day tanks rasterised its whole 96 × 40 dot display every frame to find nothing had changed, 150–195 µs a frame each. Now keyed on what it shows first: 17–29 µs.
- **Small shared fixes:** `plantReadingOf` worked out once per world step (it was recomputed by every display that reads the plant, every frame); the balance tank panel's view once per step (146 → 8 µs); the alarm printer's ribbon reshaped only when the paper moves (129 → 2 µs); the diaphragm pumps' LCDs paint only the dots that change (canvas drawing calls 15,120 → 53 a second for a pair); the mimic boards set a lamp's colour only when it changes.
- **Flagged, for the owner:** the touch panels of missions 093 (dev47, dev48) each re-upload their whole canvas about 60 times a second, 10–70 million texels a second each, with nothing changing; harmless in two development rooms, but they must redraw on change before any goes into the game. The peristaltic pump's screen (dev44 only) redraws 26 times a second for its rotor animation. Both are in `TODO.md` with proposals. Nothing was deleted or replaced.
- **Left, with their cost noted:** the strip chart recorders (a 160 × 160 chart at 4 Hz, 0.13 M texels a second: cheap), the sand filters' sight glasses (13 redraws a second while their beds churn, 0.87 M texels a second each, seven of them), POOLMON's screen (1.7 uploads a second).

## What was done with each: fix, flag or leave

**Fixed** (behaviour and appearance unchanged; each display's canvas compared pixel for pixel before and after over 600 frames, identical but for the circular chart's antialiasing, see `component-cost-2026-10-27/displays-before.jpg` and `displays-after.jpg`):

- _Circular chart recorder_ (`industrial-control/views/period-instruments.ts`): a pen's ink is laid, and the chart uploaded, only once the pen has moved a pixel. A week's chart creeps a fraction of a pixel a frame, so it uploads every few seconds, not 60 times a second.
- _DoseCell head_ (`plant/views/dose-cell-head.ts`): the display's inputs (page, test, litres and level as shown, or the lamps' words) are compared before the 96 × 40 dot frame is built; the dot key stays as the second guard.
- _`plantReadingOf`_ (`gallery/logic/world-reading.ts`): memoised in a `WeakMap` by world and pool. The world is immutable, so a reading holds until the next step; the chart recorders, readout board, mimics, annunciator, POOLMON, pump panels, pump sets, UV units, dosing boards and sand filters share it. A test checks the cache.
- _Olympic balance tank panel_ (`gallery/views/room-equipment.ts`): `olympicTankView` once per world step instead of once for each of its eight readings every frame.
- _Teleprinter (alarm printer)_ (`industrial-control/views/teleprinter.ts`): its paper ribbon (297 vertices and their normals) reshaped only when the paper moves.
- _Diaphragm dosing pumps' LCDs_ (`plant/views/diaphragm-pump.ts`): only the dots that changed are painted, each over its own square (a stroke marker is 16 dots of 4,000); the whole matrix only when it switches on or off. The upload is unchanged (the picture did change).
- _Mimic boards_ (`industrial-control/views/mimic-board.ts`): a lamp's colour is parsed and set only when it changes (it was a CSS colour string parsed for every lamp every frame). Checked against `room-power.ts`, which darkens lamps in a power cut and remembers colours set meanwhile: it still sees each change.

**Flagged for the owner** (in `TODO.md`):

- _The touch panels_ (mission 093, dev47 and dev48, not yet in the game): every panel uploads its whole canvas about 60 times a second with nothing changing, 10–70 M texels a second each (the sidebar frame 69 M; dev48's 69 panels together would be about 2 G texels a second). Proposed: the `ui2d` tree marks itself dirty and the surface redraws and uploads only then, with animations (blinking, sweeps) at no more than 10 Hz; before any panel goes into a room. Left to mission 093's owner, whose files these are.
- _The peristaltic dosing pump's screen_ (dev44 only): 26 redraws a second of a 480 × 288 canvas (230,000 canvas calls a second) because the rotor's phase is in its key; 400–530 µs a frame. Proposed: a still RUN glyph on the screen (the rotor turns in 3D, and is heard), or a simpler segment display after the diaphragm pumps', before it is used in a room.

**Left**, cheap enough or showing a real change: the strip chart recorders, the sand filters' sight glasses, POOLMON's screen, the CCTV monitor (as measured), the meters of dev43 and the level instruments of dev44 (each at 60 uploads a second only because their demonstrations drive them every frame; in the game the world drives them at most ten times a second), the chemical transfer trial rig of dev44 (2.3 ms a frame, a development rig only, its DoseCell heads and diaphragm pumps now cheaper).

## Budgets

`logic/component-bench.ts` holds a budget per component (`DEFAULT_BUDGET`): with the world still, at most 12 uploads and 250,000 texels a second (a display animating on its own, a strip chart's paper or a blinking cursor, may upload a few times a second); running, at most 300,000 texels a second; an update at most 200 µs. The benchmark lists every component over it at the top of its report, and the table's last column says why. Before the fixes, of the components the rooms use: the chart recorders, the sand filters (their sight glasses), the operator's desk, the wet hall mimic, the spa and jacuzzi packs while running, the first boiler cascade, the hypochlorite dosing pumps and the pump panel; all the touch panels; the dev44 peristaltic pump, transfer trial and day tank sizes. After: the chart recorders are within it; the sight glasses, POOLMON, and the packs remain (each a real animation, noted above). Failing loudly in CI is not done: the numbers need a GPU and a browser, and vary by machine (see caveats). Proposed instead: rerun the benchmark after any new display and before a release, and treat a component over budget as a bug.

## Proposed rule for `CLAUDE.md` (stage 4, not applied)

For the owner to approve, worded as the other rules are:

> Displays redraw only when what they show changes (mission 094): a canvas is drawn and its texture uploaded only when its inputs (its values, page, power, or an animation's phase rounded to what can be seen) differ from those it last drew, compared before any drawing; view state derived from the world is worked out once per world step (`store.read`, or a cache keyed on the world object), not by every display every frame. A display that animates on its own does so at no more than 10 Hz. New displays are run through the component benchmark (`bench.html?bench=components&only=<name>`) and kept within its budget.

## Method

- **The benchmark** (`src/toolkit/gallery/views/component-bench.ts`, pure parts in `logic/component-bench.ts`, page `bench.html`, development only and not in the build). The candidates: every distinct item key placed in the 13 equipped rooms (`EQUIPPED_ROOMS`, `layoutRoom`), each built by the room's own `modelFor` (`buildRoomItem`) for the first room it appears in; the Wash-O-Tron (placed by the Olympic plant room outside its item list); the circular and strip chart recorders on their own; every exhibit of 17 component development rooms not already used by a room (pumps, balance, chemicals, dosing, boiler, air, electrical, control, ozone, services, pool fittings, dev38, dev39, dev43, dev44, dev47, dev48); dev0's 16 boards and its 27 plinths. 339 in all; every one built.
- **Each, alone**, in an 800 × 600 canvas with an ambient and a directional light, framed from in front, its own `WebGLRenderer` (`createRenderer`, pixel ratio 1, no post-processing). Build timed; 0.3 s of frames to warm (shaders, first uploads); then 1.2 s of frames with the world's clock stopped (`clockStopped`: idle, nothing it shows changes), its update called back to back 120 times (precise: Chrome's frame timer is coarse), and 1.2 s with the world running at 1× (active). Each frame: its update (the room's way: its parts, then its own), then `renderer.render`, each timed.
- **Counted** by wrapping the renderer's WebGL context: level-0 `texImage2D` and `texSubImage2D` calls are uploads, their sizes are texels (from the call's arguments or its source canvas); and by wrapping `CanvasRenderingContext2D`'s drawing calls (fills, strokes, text, images). Draw calls and triangles from `renderer.info` for one frame; materials and textures (and their size, RGBA8 with mip chains) by walking the object.
- **Ranked** by unit cost (active update + own render − the empty scene's render) × copies (busy rooms twice); counts are from the room layouts (a key with an argument, `chem-day:acid:pool_01`, counts as itself). Fix numbers are from a second run of the affected components with the same settings.

## Caveats

- **One machine.** An Apple M3 Max (unified memory, a very fast GPU) in Chrome 150 through ANGLE on Metal. Texture uploads there cost the main thread almost nothing (the copy happens in the GPU process), which is why the circular chart's 15.7 M texels a second hardly showed in its render time; on an integrated GPU or a weaker laptop the same uploads cost bandwidth and can stall. Draw-call cost (about 15 µs each here) is higher on slower CPUs. Rank order should hold; absolute numbers will not.
- **Noise.** Other agents' game tabs and the development server ran during the runs; render times varied by ±30 % between runs (compare a component's before and after render columns). The back-to-back update figure is the steadiest; the per-frame mean and worst are quantised by Chrome's 0.1 ms timer (the page is not cross-origin isolated). The pump panel measured 228 µs in both runs in the benchmark but 27–36 µs built and timed alone in the console; the cause is unknown (perhaps where it falls in the run), so its update figure is uncertain.
- **Not as the room draws it.** No post-processing (GTAO, bloom), no shadows, no room around it, no doorway; draw calls are the component's own, unmerged (the plant rooms do not merge their machines either). GPU time was not measured (no timer query).
- **Not measured:** the CCTV monitor with its camera running (the camera is the water room's, not built by the benchmark; the research's per-frame upload and `readPixels` stall need a room-level measurement with the water room live); the room-level work outside the item lists (pipework, services, lights, `room-lights.ts` and `room-power.ts` scans); the yard's meters; the pool halls' own fittings.
- **Copies** come from the equipped rooms' layouts only; a component also placed elsewhere (a dev room, the yard) counts only there. A key built for its first room stands for its copies in others (a lounge pump panel for the Olympic's, for example), though they bind to their own pools.
- **The drivers.** Room items are driven by the world as it starts in a fresh `bench094` slot (all plant running); the chart recorders on their own by a fast-swinging test signal, far faster than the hall air changes in the game, so their "run" upload figures are an upper bound. Development exhibits run their own demonstrations, often stepping every frame.
