# Bridge rendering cost, 2026-10-28

The bridge at `?room=bridge` was profiled after pulling source `110913d`. The main renderer uses a scene pass, GTAO ambient occlusion, bloom, output and film. GTAO also renders the room’s geometry into its normal buffer. At the measured view, the scene held 1,148 meshes, 328 materials and 115 textures. The full effects chain made about 815 draw calls per frame; a single scene render made about 398. Seven point lights and one spot light were present; none cast a shadow map. The live displays repainted about 216 canvases and uploaded about 92–140 changed textures per second in sampled intervals. That is ongoing work, but GTAO gave the largest measured change in frame rate.

In an isolated headless Chrome using ANGLE Metal on the host Apple M5, I opened the bridge at a fixed 1280 × 713 CSS viewport, set its renderer to 2× pixel ratio (2560 × 1426 render target), and compared the same scene with GTAO at 12, 6 and 4 samples. Each case ran for about three seconds after shader warm-up; the bridge’s existing bloom and other effects remained on. At 12 samples the measured rate was about 47 fps, with the 95th-percentile frame interval about 33 ms. At 6 samples it was about 58 fps, with the 95th-percentile interval about 17 ms. Four samples gave about 58 fps too, so six was the better visual compromise. With GTAO disabled entirely, a prior comparison reached 60 fps. These are short, local measurements, not a guarantee of a particular rate on the owner’s display or other GPUs.

At the bridge’s stair landing, 1280 × 713 screenshots with 12 and 6 samples were visually similar: the darkening under consoles and at wall/floor joins remained. The isolated images are temporary local files `/tmp/pool-bridge-ao12.png` and `/tmp/pool-bridge-ao6.png`. The six-sample setting is restricted to `cfg.self === 'bridge'` in `src/toolkit/gallery/rooms/shell-room.ts`; other rooms retain twelve samples. Existing render-quality settings and the bridge’s remaining effects are unchanged.

Further cost reductions, if needed, are lower-resolution GTAO and presets that disable AO at low quality. Those require separate visual and performance checks because GTAO’s normal, AO and denoise buffers currently all use the composer’s full pixel size. The existing graphics-preset research at `doc/research/graphics-quality-presets.md` covers that broader work.

Validation: typecheck, lint (existing warnings), format check and build 0.1.660 passed. Full suite: 3,010 pass, 10 skip, one known Wash-O-Tron AUTO timeout under parallel load; the seven tests in that file passed separately. The TODO retains the timing follow-up and a check on other GPUs.
