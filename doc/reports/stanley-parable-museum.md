# The Stanley Parable's museum, and what PLANTROOMS could take from it

Research report, 2026-10-30. The owner says The Stanley Parable's museum inspired ours (`museum0` to `museum9`). This report collects what is documented about it, separates fact from inference, and ends with concrete ideas for our halls. No game code was changed.

## Documented facts

### How you get there

The museum hangs off the Mind Control Facility entrance, near the end of the game's main, obedient route: instead of going in as told, Stanley takes a dim corridor to the left marked "Escape" ([Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending)). The corridor is long and narrow with six ceiling lights ([Escape hall](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Escape_hall)). Unusually for this game, the Narrator leaves the door behind open and keeps reminding the player they can turn back; if they do, he says Stanley had "too much to live for", and if they go in again he retracts it ([Escape hall](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Escape_hall)).

At its end the lights dim and a trapdoor drops Stanley into a cage on a conveyor carrying him towards a crusher, the "metal jaws", while the Narrator delivers a contemptuous farewell ([Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending)). As the jaws close they stop, and a second, female narrator interrupts: she describes the death that was about to happen, notes Stanley would be back in his office a few minutes later anyway, opens a hatch, and drops him onto a catwalk ([Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending); [Female Narrator](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Female_Narrator)). The catwalk leads left (the right is blocked by cardboard boxes) into a pitch-dark room with "THE STANLEY PARABLE" lettered overhead, and past it into the museum ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum); [Female Narrator](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Female_Narrator)).

In the original 2011 Half-Life 2 mod the same narrator appeared, but there was only a short "EXIT" corridor of the mod's props, not a museum ([Female Narrator](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Female_Narrator); [Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending)). The museum as a place arrived with the 2013 HD Remix and was carried into Ultra Deluxe (2022) ([Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending)).

### Layout and sequence

- You enter at the top of the central room, with a staircase down on each side; the doorway you came through disappears behind you once you pass a trigger, so there is no going back ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).
- The central room holds the office's nature paintings, Stanley's computer, a scale model of the office, filing cabinets, office computers, office doors and a desk, the button-sounds exhibit and the credits ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).
- From it a left-hand hall and a right-hand hall (the right with a lower floor) lead through a run of themed side rooms, and the two halls meet at the end at the exit ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). The wiki's gallery names the rooms: entry, central room, left-side hall, Warzone room, Narrator emails room, Apartment timer room, Maintenance layout room, Stanley's office evolution room, Zending room, Trailers room, right-side hall, right-side hall lower floor, Narrator outtakes room, exit ([Museum/Gallery](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum/Gallery)).
- The wiki counts 35 exhibits ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). The fan wiki does not give the room-by-room order of the two halls' contents (its own layout section is left unfinished), so the walking order beyond "central room, then either hall, then the shared exit" is not documented.

### The exhibits, in brief

Each is a prop or picture on a plinth or wall with a plaque: a title, often a sentence or two of developer explanation ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). Grouped by kind:

- **Real props from the game, shown as specimens.** Nature paintings, Stanley's computer (its terminal showing an idle prompt), filing cabinets, three office computers on pillars (each can be switched off, as in the office), office doors of employees 001 to 003 (001 exists only here and in one other room), an office clock on a pillar, lounge props, meeting-room props, a pencil sharpener and desk phone. Most of these plaques say only "THE OFFICE": the joke is in treating dull props reverently ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).
- **How the game was designed.** A blueprint model of the office to the two doors, with plaques saying the two-doors room was the first concrete piece of design and the rest grew from it, and that the opening corridor was moved to get the pacing right; a maintenance-layout board of the options discussed for the hallways after the doors, the simplest winning; the level William Pugh sent Davey Wreden as an audition piece, which got him hired; Stanley's office in three full-size versions side by side (November 2011, March 2012, February 2013) ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).
- **Cut and changed content.** Warzone, a cut ending where Stanley fights aliens, with a plaque admitting it was too jokey and on the nose, staged with a barbed-wire fence and a bike; the Apartment timer (a 15-second phone choice that was removed); two cargo-lift versions; the Zending as a screenshot, its three coloured levers (you pull one, the Narrator named its colour) and a model of its fourth version, abandoned shortly before launch; an early countdown desk whose button rises from a hatch; the monitor-room lift that once went up to one ending and down to another, dropped because players could not remember which was which; an "Escape menu" ending players never realised they had to restart from; early screenshots of the maintenance room, lounge, boss's office, underground and freedom ending ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).
- **Interactive and audio pieces.** Button sounds: five numbered buttons that each play one of the game's button sounds, with a plaque explaining each is a keystroke mixed with a synthesised tone (no on-screen prompt says they can be pressed). Narration outtakes: an empty room with a swinging lamp, eight pillars and four chairs, where unused takes by Kevan Brighting play over one another with an echo, quietly and without subtitles. Narrator emails: a projector cycling at least 20 fan emails to the Narrator. Trailers: a room showing the first teaser of May 2012, silently. Credits on five large panels ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).
- **Version-aware exhibits.** One plaque differs by game: the HD Remix shows the September 2012 Steam Greenlight campaign, Ultra Deluxe its December 2018 announcement and delay videos ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). In the HD Remix, pressing the green-leaf painting swaps in a photo of developer Jesús Higueras, everywhere that picture appears in the game ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).

### Lighting and materials

The museum is "cast in an all-white glow", white floors, walls and ceilings, lit by ceiling lights and skylights, with Ionic pillars by the walls and column railings on the stairs; the only colour is in the objects and framed pictures ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). It is approached through a pitch-black room and left through a black, dark corridor under an EXIT sign ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). Lamerichs reads the white space as a white cube, tying the game to high art ([Lamerichs](https://nicollelamerichs.com/2019/08/10/game-theory-the-meaning-of-the-stanley-parable-the-museum-ending-and-the-female-narrator/)).

### Narration and voice

The Female Narrator was voiced by Kim Hoffmeister in the mod and Lesley Staples in the HD Remix and Ultra Deluxe; the game's files call her "FemNarr"; her subtitles are light blue in the HD Remix and white in Ultra Deluxe; fans call her "The Curator", which is not canon ([Female Narrator](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Female_Narrator)). Lamerichs hears a British voice, like a critic or scholar, and notes the resemblance to the narrator of Stranger than Fiction, which the wiki also suggests as a source ([Lamerichs](https://nicollelamerichs.com/2019/08/10/game-theory-the-meaning-of-the-stanley-parable-the-museum-ending-and-the-female-narrator/); [Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending)).

She speaks very little, and only at thresholds. The dialogue page lists two museum cues: one on entering, a few sentences on why death means nothing when every path was made in advance, ending "Do you see now?"; and one on reaching the exit, which opens with a laugh and "Oh, look at these two" ([Museum/Dialogue](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum/Dialogue)). Between them, through all 35 exhibits, she is silent: the exhibits speak through plaques, and the only voices are the outtakes room's muffled Narrator and the button sounds ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)).

### Tone, the exit and the ending

The plaques are plain, sincere and a little self-deprecating (the Warzone plaque admits a mistake; the Escape-menu plaque admits players were frustrated), while the setting itself is the joke: a gallery of filing cabinets labelled "THE OFFICE" ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). The voice, by contrast, is grave and analytical, then urgent ([Female Narrator](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Female_Narrator)).

The exit is a dark room with an ON/OFF lever. At the exit she speaks of how Stanley and the Narrator want to destroy, control and free themselves of one another, and need each other; pulling the lever returns Stanley to the cage, seconds before the jaws, while she begs the player to quit the game ("Push 'escape', and press 'quit'"), in Ultra Deluxe even to switch the console off. If the player waits, Stanley is crushed mid-sentence and the game restarts; in the HD Remix it must be restarted by hand ([Museum Ending](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum_Ending); [Female Narrator](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Female_Narrator); [Lamerichs](https://nicollelamerichs.com/2019/08/10/game-theory-the-meaning-of-the-stanley-parable-the-museum-ending-and-the-female-narrator/)). Lamerichs calls it the only ending the player must shut down personally ([Lamerichs](https://nicollelamerichs.com/2019/08/10/game-theory-the-meaning-of-the-stanley-parable-the-museum-ending-and-the-female-narrator/)). The "red button" in the brief is not part of the museum as documented: the interactive pieces are the five white button-sound buttons, the computers' switches, the Zending levers, the countdown desk's button and the exit lever.

### Ultra Deluxe: the Grand Exhibit

Ultra Deluxe added the Reassurance Bucket, a companion item. Carry it down the escape hall and the museum becomes the Grand Exhibit: the same white architecture, but six exhibits on the history and majesty of buckets, written in an absurd academic deadpan (a two-handled bucket "deemed too dangerous", a photograph of 25 buckets that left the photographer in shock, a "Stress Bucket" presented without commentary, a cave painting) ([Grand exhibit](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Grand_exhibit)). The bucket is taken from you in the dark entry room and is seen hauled up through a hole in the centre into a white void; the last exhibit is your bucket hanging in the air, and taking it back is the way out, after which the narrator urges you to let Stanley be crushed so the bucket can be saved ([Grand exhibit](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Grand_exhibit)). Its placards are set in Palladio ([Grand exhibit](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Grand_exhibit)). Ultra Deluxe also updated the ordinary museum: more fan emails, the Greenlight plaque swapped for the Ultra Deluxe announcements, and the credits changed ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). Ultra Deluxe was rebuilt in Unity rather than Source ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-stanley-parable-ultra-deluxe-review/)).

### Building it

Documented: the 2013 game was a Source-engine game grown from a Half-Life 2 mod, and Ultra Deluxe moved to Unity ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-stanley-parable-ultra-deluxe-review/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/behind-the-scenes-with-i-the-stanley-parable-i-)). The vanishing entry doorway is a trigger ([Museum](https://thestanleyparable.fandom.com/wiki/Museum)). I found nothing published on the museum's map size, lighting bake, visibility set-up or performance.

## Inference (not documented)

- Source of 2013 lit static levels with precomputed lightmaps (VRAD) and cut visibility by BSP leaves and areaportals (VVIS). A white, skylit museum suits that well: soft bounced light bakes cheaply and looks expensive. The black rooms at both ends would also act as visibility breaks and hide a level change, which is how Source games usually join maps. That the museum is a map of its own is likely but unconfirmed.
- The many small side rooms off two halls, each with one idea, would keep each view short, good for both visibility culling and the player's attention.
- The silence between the two narrated thresholds looks deliberate: after a whole game of constant narration, its absence makes the museum feel like stepping outside the game.
- From the gallery's 14 named spaces and 35 exhibits, the museum is probably a few minutes' walk, far smaller than our ten halls.

## What Pool Plant's museum could learn from it

What we already have, from the code and docs: ten halls in a ring (`museum0` Welcome to `museum9` The whole plant), entrance panels and lectern placards (`src/toolkit/gallery/views/museum-exhibit.ts`), approach spotlights and black-box halls (Hall 7, `src/toolkit/gallery/rooms/museum/hall-7-grout.ts`), voice scripts for Halls 5, 7 and 8 (`doc/scripts/museum-narration.md`), lectern buttons that play a chapter with its picture light up (Hall 8), museum showcases, a shut fire exit and a freight lift in Hall 0, and the PLANTROOMS brand with its wink at the Poolrooms (`doc/scripts/museum.md`). The ideas below are suggestions for the owner, each mapped to a hall.

1. **Treat dull plant as specimens, labelled with a straight face.** Their best joke is a lit plinth of filing cabinets labelled "THE OFFICE". Hall 6 (World of pumps) and Hall 9 (The whole plant) could each have one or two plinths of the plant's humblest real parts, taken from the game's own models (a strainer basket, a gasket, a pressure gauge, a valve handwheel), each placard only "THE PLANT ROOM" and the maker's name. Hall 7 already does reverence; this is the drier, silent form of it.
2. **A design-history hall about the game itself.** Hall 8's old title slot was "Coming soon" and the brief keeps halls flexible: a hall (or one wall of Hall 9) of Pool Plant's own making, the way theirs shows Stanley's office in three dated versions. We have the material: the first blank-shell rooms, dev rooms such as dev51 and dev52, layout iterations from the plumber and scorer, the pool loop that "makes no three-dimensional sense on purpose". Framed screenshots with dated placards, an honest plaque on something cut, and the room's own development room shown as a scale model.
3. **Buttons that just play sounds.** Their five button-sound buttons cost nothing and are irresistible. A lectern in Hall 6 with numbered buttons, each playing one pump or valve sound the game already uses (a pump starting, a valve closing, water hammer, a cavitating impeller), with a placard on what makes the noise, would suit our "every interface is real" rule. Add an obvious press cue, since they found players did not know theirs could be pressed.
4. **An outtakes room.** Their quietest room, unused takes overlapping in an empty space with one swinging lamp, is the most atmospheric. Once our voice lines are recorded (`doc/scripts/museum-narration.md`), spare takes and the cut skimmer finds (`EXTRA_FINDS`) could murmur in a small black box off Hall 7 or 8, low, overlapping, unsubtitled, as set decoration. It would also use the recordings we have to commission anyway.
5. **Narrate thresholds, not exhibits.** Theirs speaks only on entering and leaving, and the long silence between is what makes it feel different. Our Hall 7 script already has `enter` and `exit`; consider making every hall's voice a threshold voice, and keeping the exhibits to placards, with Hall 8's push-button chapters as the deliberate exception where the visitor asks to hear.
6. **A voice the visitor has not heard before, at the end.** Their twist is a new narrator. Hall 9 or Hall 0's return could bring a second voice, distinct from Hall 7's reverent guide and Hall 8's documentary narrator, that speaks about the museum and the plant rooms from outside, briefly and once, on the way out. Kept dry and sincere, never winking, to match the museum's register.
7. **A dark room at each end.** They frame the white museum between a pitch-black entry with the title lettered overhead and a black exit corridor. Our freight lift arrives in Hall 0: a short dark lobby between the lift and the hall, with PLANTROOMS lettered over it, would give the same arrival, and would also hide the hall's loading behind the lift ride (Mission 108's timings show it is long enough).
8. **The exit as an object.** Their way out is a lever you choose to pull. Hall 0's fire exit is shut set decoration and the lift's call button already exists; the lift car's panel is the natural "lever": the museum ends when the visitor chooses a floor. A closing card or line could sit on the landing, as their exit room does.
9. **One exhibit that changes the world.** Pressing their leaf painting changed every copy of it across the game. A Hall 9 lectern that sets something persistent in the world state (say, which mimic board in the old control room carries a small museum-donated plaque, or the plant room's poster of the month) would tie the museum to the game behind it. It needs a `WORLD_VERSION` bump if it is saved.
10. **A carried-object variant.** The Grand Exhibit rewrites the whole museum when you carry a bucket. We already have exhibit carrying (`src/toolkit/gallery/rooms/museum/exhibit-carrying.ts`) and the skimmer finds. Carrying something absurd into Hall 0 (the skimmer's rat, a pool float) could swap the title wall and a few placards for a reverent history of that object, the way Hall 7 reveres grout. Small cost: the words only, the halls unchanged.
11. **Silent video and a model.** Their trailers room plays a teaser with no sound, and a blueprint model sits in the central room. A small architectural model of the leisure centre and its plant rooms in Hall 9, built from `facility-plan.ts`, would let the visitor see the whole building at once, which the game never otherwise shows.
12. **White as a contrast, not a default.** Their museum is white because the game around it is a grey office. Ours is painted brick and polished concrete, with black boxes for focus. Keep that, but one all-white, skylit hall (Hall 9, or the finale's end wall) would mark it as the place where the visitor steps back and looks at the whole.

Performance notes from their approach that fit our open items in `TODO.md` ("Drawing the museum for less"): dark threshold rooms and narrow side rooms limit what can be seen at once, which is what our proposed screen walls in each archway would do; and their baked, even white light is a reminder that light washes or baked pools are cheaper than our per-hall point and spot lights.
